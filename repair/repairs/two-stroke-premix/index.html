Repair

Mix two-stroke pre-mix oil and petrol

Rider jobNo special toolsTime: 5 min (estimate)

Some two-stroke engines are lubricated by oil mixed into the petrol. The mix is written as parts of petrol to one part of oil, for example 20 to 1.

Safety

  • Petrol is flammable. Mix outdoors, away from flames, in a can made for fuel.

Choosing the ratio

  • Use the oil and ratio the engine maker states. A maker's oil in the maker's engine at the maker's ratio is the safe choice.
  • If you use a different brand of oil, or the ratio is unknown, a richer mix such as 20 to 1 is the safer side. Oils differ in thickness and additives, and the maker's ratio was set for their own oil.
  • A richer mix can foul the spark plug and block the spark arrester or silencer with oily carbon. A mix that is too lean risks a seized piston.
  • The carburettor jets are fixed. Oil is not petrol, so changing the ratio changes the air and fuel mixture slightly. A big change in ratio may need a jet change.
  • A worn engine burns oil badly, and a worn crankshaft seal lets gearbox oil in. Both foul plugs. Check the seals before blaming the ratio.

How to work out the oil

Oil per litre of petrol, in millilitres, is 1000 divided by the ratio. For example:

Ratio Oil per litre of petrol
20 to 1 50 ml
25 to 1 40 ml
40 to 1 25 ml
50 to 1 20 ml

A measuring cup with ratio marks makes this easy.

Steps

  1. Pour the right amount of oil into a clean fuel can.
  2. Add the petrol and shake the can to mix.
  3. Fill the tank with the mix. Do not store a mix for months.

Check your work

  • The ratio on the can matches the manual.
  • The plug is clean tan or brown after a ride.

If it did not fix the problem

  • Spark plug is wet with oil.
  • Free a spark arrester blocked with oily carbon.

Tools

  • measuring cup with ratio marks
  • clean fuel can
  • funnel

Consumables

  • two-stroke oil
  • fresh petrol
