Use an air or cordless impact wrench safely

Home workshopBasic tools and some experienceTime: 5 to 15 min per job (estimate)

An impact wrench (also called an air gun or rattle gun) hits the socket with many fast blows. It can undo a tight nut, such as a flywheel or clutch nut, without locking the engine, because the blows are too quick for the crankshaft to turn. It is a removal tool. It has far too much power and no feel for tightening motorcycle fasteners.

Safety

  • Wear eye protection. Sockets, broken bolts and rust flakes can fly off.
  • Use only impact sockets. An ordinary chrome socket can split under the blows and throw pieces.
  • Keep fingers away from the socket and the work. The tool kicks when the nut breaks loose.
  • Air tools: use a hose and fittings rated for your compressor pressure. Do not point the air line at skin.

You need

  • An impact wrench in the right drive size. Small drive sizes suit small fasteners; a large gun can twist a small bolt in half.
  • Six point impact sockets that fit the nut snugly: Impact sockets.
  • For an air gun, enough air volume. A small compressor may run the gun only for a few seconds. An extra air tank in the line helps.

Steps

  1. Soak rusty threads with penetrating oil first and wait.
  2. Check the thread direction. Some nuts are left-hand thread; the manual lists them: Identify bolt strength, thread and locking types.
  3. Set the tool to reverse (undo). Start on the lowest power setting.
  4. Push the socket fully onto the nut and hold the tool square to it.
  5. Give short bursts. If the nut does not move after a few seconds, stop. More hammering can damage threads or a crankshaft key. Try more power, heat or a breaker bar: Undo a stuck or rounded fastener.
  6. Once loose, you may spin the nut off with the gun, or by hand.

Refitting

  • Start every nut and bolt by hand so it cannot cross thread.
  • You may run a nut down a long thread with the gun at low power, but stop before it seats.
  • Do the final tightening with hand tools and a torque wrench to the value in the manual: Use a torque wrench.

Check your work

  • The fastener came off with its thread and head undamaged.
  • On refit, every fastener was tightened by hand and torque wrench, not by the gun.

If it did not fix the problem

  • Rounded head or broken bolt: Bolt or screw head is rounded or stuck.
  • Thread pulled out of the casting: A bolt hole thread is stripped.

Tools

  • impact wrench (air or battery)
  • impact sockets
  • eye protection
  • ear protection
  • torque wrench

Consumables

  • penetrating oil
