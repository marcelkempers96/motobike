Check the airbox inlet, filter and breather for a blockage

Rider jobNo special toolsTime: 10 to 20 min (estimate)

A blocked air supply makes the engine suck too much fuel for the air it gets. The result is a rich mixture: wasted fuel, black smoke and a carbon-fouled spark plug. Dirt is the usual cause, but things can also block the airbox inlet from outside or from inside. Check this before touching the carburettor jets.

Safety

  • Engine off and cool.
  • Do not store loose items under the seat if the airbox inlet is there.

Steps

  1. Look at the airbox inlet and any ducts. Make sure nothing is stuffed or pressed against it, such as a cloth under the seat.
  2. Open the airbox. Look for dust, leaves, nests and insects. Mice and wasps can build nests inside the airbox.
  3. Inspect the filter. - Paper: hold it to a light. If it is dark, oily, or wet, replace it. Paper that has got wet passes less air even after it dries, and a bike that has stood a long time can absorb moisture. Blowing with air cleans it only a little. - Foam or fabric: see Clean and re-oil a foam air filter.
  4. Check the crankcase breather. A worn engine can push oil vapour through the breather and into the airbox, and this can plug the filter. A filter on the breather hose, with the hose blocked off where it enters the airbox, may be needed. See your manual.
  5. Check the air tube between airbox and carburettor for cracks and splits. See Replace a hard, shrunk air tube.
  6. Refit the filter and cover so the seal is flat.

Check your work

  • Clear airbox with a clean, dry filter.
  • Plug colour improves and the exhaust smoke stops after a test ride.

If it did not fix the problem

  • Read a spark plug.
  • Check the choke or starter valve.
  • Engine runs rich.

Tools

  • screwdriver
  • torch
  • clean cloth
