Adjust a manual cam chain tensioner

Workshop jobCheck safely, then hand it to a workshopTime: 30 to 60 min (estimate)

The cam chain connects the crankshaft to the camshaft. A tensioner keeps it tight as it wears and stretches. Newer engines use an automatic tensioner that needs no adjustment. Older engines have a manual one with a lock bolt or screw. When the chain rattles and the engine is older, a correct adjustment may stop the noise. When it does not, the chain is worn and needs replacing.

Safety

  • Engine off, cold and in neutral before setting any mark.
  • Keep fingers and clothing away from the chain and sprockets if the engine must idle for the adjustment.
  • Do not over tighten. A tight chain wears fast, loads bearings and stops the engine from revving.

Types of tensioner

  • Manual: a shaft, spring and lock bolt or lock nut set the tension.
  • Automatic: spring pressure, sometimes oil pressure, sets the tension. A one-way lock stops it backing off.
  • The part that pushes the chain is a sprocket, a rubber roller or a flexible plate, usually at the rear of the engine.

Steps

  1. Find the tensioner and its lock bolt. Check the manual for the right position of the engine and whether it must idle.
  2. Turn the engine by hand to the mark on the flywheel given in the manual. This brings the chain slack to the tensioner side.
  3. Loosen the lock bolt. The spring takes up the slack. On a worn chain a faint pop or click is normal.
  4. Tighten the lock bolt. On a screw type, set the tension screw as the manual states, then tighten the lock nut.
  5. If the manual calls for adjusting with the engine idling, keep clear of moving parts and use the lock nut.
  6. Refit any cap, then start the engine and listen. The rattle should be gone.

If you refit a tensioner

  • Fit it in the loosest position, with no pressure on the chain. Release the spring, release the one-way lock if there is one, and push the shaft to its loosest point.
  • A tensioner fitted under pressure over tightens the chain. It can break the tensioner or limit engine speed.

Check your work

  • No chain rattle at idle or on throttle.
  • The engine revs freely.
  • Oil is not leaking at the tensioner.

If the noise stays

  • If adjusting does not remove the noise, the chain is worn out: Check the cam chain and tensioner.
  • A stronger spring or locked tensioner is a short-term fix. The lasting fix is a new chain, and new sprockets if worn.
  • A worn chain can jump a tooth and let valves hit the piston, or cut into the case and put metal in the oil.
  • Rattle from the top of the engine may also be valve clearance: Check and adjust valve clearance.

When to go to a workshop

  • The chain is still noisy after adjustment.
  • The sprocket is fixed to the crankshaft and cannot be replaced alone.
  • Timing marks have moved or are not clear: Record and set cam timing marks.

Tools

  • spanners
  • screwdriver
  • torch
  • shop manual
  • stethoscope or long screwdriver

Consumables

  • tensioner gasket or sealing washer if one is fitted
