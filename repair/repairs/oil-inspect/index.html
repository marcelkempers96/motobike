Inspect drained oil for clues

Home workshopBasic tools and some experienceTime: 10 to 15 min (estimate, during an oil change) (estimate)

Home mechanic (basic tools) · Time: 10 to 15 min (estimate, during an oil change) (estimate)

Old oil tells a story about the inside of the engine. A few minutes of looking during an oil change can warn you about a failing bearing, a fuel or coolant leak, or a clutch that is wearing out. What you find is a clue, not proof. Confirm a fault with other checks before you open the engine.

Safety

  • Wear nitrile gloves. Do not taste or sniff oil from close up for long.
  • Hot oil burns. Let it cool in the tray before you put your fingers in it.

You need

  • A clean glass jar, a strong magnet, a white paper towel and a torch.

Steps

  1. While the oil drains, catch some of the first oil in the clean jar.
  2. Hold the jar to the light. Note the colour: amber to brown is normal; black is old or very sooty; grey or milky means water or coolant.
  3. Smell it gently from a short distance. A sharp petrol smell means fuel is getting into the oil.
  4. Drip some oil onto the white paper towel. Look at the spot after a minute. Fine sparkle, grit or a dark ring of sludge are worth noting.
  5. Look closely at the strainer screen and the drain cap. On many engines the cap or plug has a magnet; look for a furry coat of metal on it.
  6. Run the magnet through the bottom of the tray. Steel particles stick; aluminium and bronze do not.
  7. Look at the size of what you find. Very fine grey paste is normal wear. Visible flakes, slivers, chips or pieces of rubber or plastic are not.
  8. Let the jar stand for an hour. Water sinks and forms a separate layer or droplets at the bottom.
  9. Write down what you found and the odometer reading. Compare at the next change.

What it can mean

Check your work

  • You kept a sample or a photo if you found something unusual. A workshop can use it.
  • You cleaned the screen and magnet before refitting.

If it did not fix the problem

  • This is a diagnosis step only. For chips, flakes or large debris, do not run the engine; see Assess bottom end and internal engine damage.
