Check the crankcase breather

Home workshopBasic tools and some experienceTime: 20 to 30 min (estimate)

Home mechanic (basic tools) · Time: 20 to 30 min (estimate) (estimate)

When the piston moves, the air pressure inside the crankcase rises and falls. Some combustion gas also leaks past the piston rings (blow-by). The breather is a hose, often with a small chamber or separator, that lets this gas out without letting oil out. On most bikes it runs to the air filter box so the fumes are burned. A blocked breather builds pressure and pushes oil out of seals and gaskets. A lot of smoke or oil at the breather means heavy blow-by from engine wear.

Safety

  • Work on a cold engine.
  • Do not run the engine with the breather pulled off near your face; hot oil mist can come out.

You need

  • Torch, rags, pliers for hose clips.
  • A new hose of the same inside size if the old one is cracked or soft.

Steps

  1. Find the breather hose. It usually leaves the top of the crankcase or the head cover and goes to the air filter box.
  2. Follow the whole hose. Look for kinks, crushed sections, cracks, a missing clip or a hose that has come off.
  3. Remove the hose. Look through it against the light. Clear sludge with a rag on a wire, then blow through it. Do not use a sharp wire inside the engine fitting.
  4. Find the drain at the bottom of the air box or breather chamber (often a short clear tube with a plug). Remove the plug and let any oil and water out. Some bikes have a mark on this tube; empty it when liquid reaches the mark.
  5. Look at the air filter. Oil soaked into it from the breather side is a warning sign. Replace a soaked paper element; see Clean or replace the air filter.
  6. Check the oil level. An overfilled engine pushes oil into the breather; see Check the engine oil level.
  7. Refit the hose with clips, with no kinks and away from the exhaust.
  8. With the engine idling, hold a clean rag a little distance from the breather outlet or the air box drain. A light mist is normal. Steady smoke or puffs that pulse with the engine mean heavy blow-by.

Check your work

  • Hose clear, clipped at both ends, no kinks.
  • Air box drain empty and plugged.
  • After a ride, no fresh oil on the air filter or round seals.

If it did not fix the problem

  • Heavy smoke at the breather or oil filler: blow-by from worn rings. Go to Test engine compression. The K-Light smoke chart points to cylinder, piston and ring wear when smoke comes from the filler at high rpm.
  • Oil keeps appearing at seals: Trace an oil leak.

Tools

  • screwdriver
  • pliers
  • torch
  • rags

Consumables

  • breather hose of the right size if cracked
  • hose clips
