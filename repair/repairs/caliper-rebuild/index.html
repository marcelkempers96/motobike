Rebuild a brake caliper

Workshop jobCheck safely, then hand it to a workshopTime: 15 to 30 min assessment; rebuild time set by the workshop (estimate)

A caliper rebuild means taking the pistons out, fitting new piston seals and dust seals, and replacing pistons or the whole caliper if they are damaged. It is a workshop job: getting the pistons out uses compressed air or hydraulic pressure, and a wrongly fitted seal causes brake failure. This page tells you how to judge whether a rebuild is needed and what to ask for.

Safety

  • Do not ride if fluid is leaking from a caliper, a piston is seized, or the pads are wet with fluid.
  • Do not try to blow pistons out at home. The K-Light 125 manual uses compressed air with a thick wooden block and rubber pad in the caliper, and warns about fingers: a piston comes out with great force.
  • Do not split a two-piece caliper. The joint bolts and their seal are set at the factory (K-Light 125 rear caliper joint bolt is listed at 30 N·m, but this is workshop work).

What you can check safely

  1. Look at the caliper with a torch: wet fluid round a piston or dust boot, or a fluid trail down the fork leg or swingarm, means a seal leak.
  2. Look at the pads: fluid-soaked pads must be replaced after the repair.
  3. Check whether the pistons move, as in Clean a brake caliper and free the pistons. A piston that will not move after cleaning, or that is pitted or corroded where it enters the seal, needs a rebuild or new caliper.
  4. Note whether the bike has CBS or ABS. The workshop needs to bleed those systems in their own order.

What the workshop will do

  • Remove the caliper and the banjo bolt, keeping the hose end high so fluid does not run out. New sealing washers are fitted on both sides of the banjo bolt.
  • Remove the pads, spring plate and bracket, then the pistons.
  • Remove and replace the piston seals and dust seals. Clean the bores with brake fluid or alcohol only.
  • Inspect pistons and bores. Scored or pitted parts are replaced, or the whole caliper is replaced if parts are not sold.
  • Clean and grease slide pins and replace their boots.
  • Refit with new pads if the old ones were contaminated, tighten the banjo bolt to the model value (K-Light 125: 30 N·m) and the caliper bolts to the confirmed model torque, then bleed the system.

What to ask for

  • New seal kit for your caliper (by make, model, year and front or rear), or a new or exchange caliper.
  • New sealing washers for every banjo bolt that is opened.
  • A full bleed with fresh fluid of the type on the cap, and a CBS or ABS bleed by the maker's routine where fitted.
  • To see the old pistons and seals.

Check your work

  • Lever or pedal firm, no sinking when held.
  • No wetness at the caliper after a short ride and a few hard stops.
  • Wheel spins freely when the brake is released.

If it did not fix the problem

  • Lever still soft: Brake lever or pedal feels spongy.
  • Lever sinks with no external leak: Check the brake master cylinder.
