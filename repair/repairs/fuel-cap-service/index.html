Clean and lubricate the fuel cap

Rider jobNo special toolsTime: 10 to 20 min (estimate)

The fuel cap keeps dirt and rain out of the tank, keeps fuel in, and often holds the tank vent: a small hole or one-way valve that lets air in as fuel is used. Many caps also have a key lock. A dirty or dry cap is hard to open, a cracked seal lets water in and fuel out, and a blocked vent starves the engine.

Safety

  • No smoking or flames while the filler is open. Wipe up spilled fuel.
  • Keep dirt out of the tank: clean around the cap before you open it, and lay the cap on a clean rag.
  • Never force a stuck lock with pliers or a screwdriver. A broken cap or lock may need a new cap set, or a locksmith or dealer.

You need

  • A dry lock lubricant (graphite or PTFE). Oil and grease collect dust and gum up locks.
  • A new seal ring if the old one is cracked, flat or swollen. Some seals come only with a new cap.

Steps

  1. Wipe dust and sand from around the cap and its hinge before opening.
  2. Open the cap. Look at the rubber seal: it must be soft, whole and seated in its groove.
  3. Find the vent: a tiny hole in the cap, or a passage under a small cover held by screws. Clear dirt with a soft brush or a thin soft wire. Do not enlarge the hole.
  4. If the cap has a breather valve (a small flap or ball), blow gently through the vent: air should pass at least one way. If it is blocked and cannot be cleaned, replace the cap.
  5. Brush the lock keyway. Puff a little dry lubricant into it. Insert and turn the key several times.
  6. Put a light film of the same lubricant on the hinge and latch. Keep lubricant off the seal and out of the vent and tank.
  7. Close the cap and check it latches firmly and seals all the way round.

Check your work

  • The cap opens and locks smoothly with the key.
  • No fuel smell or wet mark around the cap after a ride.
  • No hiss of air going in when you open the cap after a ride (a hiss means the vent is still blocked: see Check the fuel tank vent).

If it did not fix the problem

  • Water keeps getting into the fuel: replace the cap seal, then see Water or dirt in the fuel.
  • Lock still stuck or the key turns without opening: the lock is worn or broken. Replace the cap set.

Tools

  • screwdriver or hex key for the cap screws
  • soft brush
  • clean rag
  • torch

Consumables

  • dry graphite or PTFE lock lubricant
  • new cap seal if cracked
