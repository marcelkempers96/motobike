Repair

Plug a tubeless tyre puncture

Rider jobNo special toolsTime: 15 to 25 min (estimate)

A plug pushed in from outside seals a small hole in the tread of a tubeless tyre so you can ride gently to a tyre shop. Treat it as a temporary repair. A lasting repair is a combined plug and patch fitted from inside the tyre by a tyre shop, to a recognised standard such as BS AU 159, or a new tyre.

Safety

  • Only plug a small hole in the central tread area. Never plug the sidewall or the shoulder (the curved edge of the tread).
  • Do not plug a tyre that was ridden flat or nearly flat. The inside may be damaged where you cannot see it. Replace it.
  • After plugging: ride gently, keep speed down, no passenger and no heavy load until the tyre is properly repaired or replaced.
  • Never fit an inner tube inside a tubeless tyre on a tubeless rim as a fix. The rough rim inside rubs the tube and it overheats.

You need

  • A tubeless repair kit for motorcycles: a reamer (spiral rasp), an insertion needle and sticky plug strings. Some kits need rubber cement.
  • Pliers, a knife, a pump or CO2 cartridges, soapy water.
  • Check first that the tyre is tubeless: the sidewall says TUBELESS or TL, and the valve is fitted in the rim, not to a tube.

Steps

  1. Support the bike so the wheel can turn. Find the hole: spray soapy water over the tread and watch for bubbles. Mark it with chalk.
  2. Check the hole is in the tread centre and small (nail sized). If not, stop: get the bike to a tyre shop.
  3. Pull the object out with pliers. Note the angle it went in.
  4. Push the reamer into the hole along the same angle and work it in and out several times. This cleans and shapes the hole.
  5. Thread a plug string through the eye of the insertion needle so it sits halfway. Coat it with cement if your kit uses it.
  6. Push the needle into the hole until about a third of the plug is still outside. Pull the needle straight out with one quick movement. The plug stays in.
  7. Cut the ends off a few millimetres above the tread. Do not pull on the plug.
  8. Inflate to the correct pressure: Check tyre pressure.
  9. Spray soapy water over the plug. No bubbles should appear.

Check your work

  • No bubbles at the plug or anywhere else on the tread.
  • Pressure is unchanged after 10 minutes and again after a short slow ride.

If it did not fix the problem

  • Still bubbling: try a second plug in the same hole once. If it still leaks, the hole is too big: do not ride on it.
  • Losing air elsewhere: check the valve and bead, see Tyre slowly loses air.
  • For the lasting repair or a new tyre: Replace a tyre.

Tools

  • tubeless repair kit with reamer and insertion tool
  • pliers
  • sharp knife
  • pump or CO2 cartridges
  • soapy water
  • chalk

Consumables

  • plug strings
  • rubber cement if the kit uses it
