Check the fuel tank vent

Rider jobNo special toolsTime: 10 to 20 min (estimate)

As fuel leaves the tank, air must come in to replace it. Air enters through a small vent: a hole in the fuel cap, or a vent hose from the tank or cap. Many newer bikes route the vent through a rollover valve and a charcoal canister (an emissions part that traps fuel vapour). If the vent blocks, a vacuum builds in the tank, fuel stops flowing and the engine dies after a few kilometres. When you open the cap you hear air rush in.

Safety

  • Engine off and cool. No flames or sparks near the open filler.
  • Never block or seal a vent to stop a smell or leak. A tank with no vent can starve the engine, and some tanks can deform.

You need

  • A torch, a thin soft wire or nylon fishing line, and a hand pump or low-pressure air. Do not use high-pressure shop air into a tank.

Steps

  1. Listen test: after the engine has stopped from starvation, open the fuel cap slowly. A clear hiss of air going in means the vent is blocked.
  2. Ride test (rider): if the bike starts again after you open the cap and runs normally while the cap is loose, the vent is the cause. Only ride a short distance this way, at low speed, with the cap closed again before you move off on public roads.
  3. Find the vent. Look under the cap for a small hole or a one-way valve. Look under the tank for a thin hose that ends at the frame or goes to a canister.
  4. Clean the cap vent. Remove dirt and dried wax with a soft wire or fine line. Do not drill or enlarge the hole. If the cap has a valve inside, see Clean and lubricate the fuel cap.
  5. Check the vent hose. It must not be kinked, crushed under the tank, blocked by mud or pinched by a clip. Blow gently through it from the free end; air should pass.
  6. Bikes with a rollover valve or canister: the K-Light 125 has a rollover ("dump") valve and an evaporative canister. A blocked canister or valve gives the same symptoms. Check the hoses for kinks. A blocked valve or canister is replaced, not cleaned; ask a workshop if you are not sure which hose is which.
  7. Refit everything and close the cap.

Check your work

  • Open the cap after a 10 to 20 km ride: little or no hiss.
  • The engine no longer dies after a few kilometres.

If it did not fix the problem

  • Engine still starves with a clear vent: see Engine stops and only runs on reserve or after a rest.
  • Strong fuel smell around the cap or vent hose: see Strong smell of fuel.

Tools

  • torch
  • thin soft wire or nylon line
  • low-pressure air (hand pump)
  • rags
