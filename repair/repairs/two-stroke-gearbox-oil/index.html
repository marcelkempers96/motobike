Change the gearbox oil on a two-stroke

Home workshopBasic tools and some experienceTime: 20 to 30 min (estimate)

A two-stroke has no oil filter and no oil pump for the crankcase. The gearbox and clutch share a separate supply of oil that is splashed around, and the engine itself is oiled by pre-mix or an oil injection pump. The engine oil is always fresh, but the gearbox oil still ages and must be changed at the interval in the manual.

Safety

  • Hot oil burns. Let the engine cool a little.
  • Keep oil off the rear tyre and the brakes.

Steps

  1. Warm the engine briefly so the oil flows, then stop it and stand the bike upright.
  2. Put the drain pan under the gearbox drain bolt. Look in the manual for the right bolt and the amount of oil.
  3. Remove the filler cap or level plug to let air in. Remove the drain bolt and let the oil drain fully.
  4. Look at the old oil for metal or milky colour. See Inspect drained oil for clues.
  5. Fit the drain bolt with a new washer and tighten to the torque in the manual. See Tighten drain and filter bolts without stripping them.
  6. Pour in the amount and type of oil the manual gives. Many engines have a level plug as well: the oil should reach that hole.
  7. Refit the cap or plug and wipe up any spills.

Check your work

  • The level is right when checked upright.
  • No drips from the drain bolt.
  • The clutch works normally and the bike pulls away without slipping.

If it did not fix the problem

  • Oil is getting into the engine and the exhaust smokes: Old two-stroke smokes heavily.
  • The gearbox level keeps rising or the oil looks thin: the crankshaft seal on the gearbox side may be leaking. See Remove and fit an engine oil seal.

Tools

  • drain pan
  • spanner or socket
  • torque wrench
  • funnel
  • clean rags

Consumables

  • gearbox oil of the type in the manual
  • new sealing washer
