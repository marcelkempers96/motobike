Brakes

Drum brake squeaks or grinds

Ride with careAlso called: drum brake squeal, scraping from hub, rear brake noise

A squeak, squeal or scraping noise from inside a wheel hub with a drum brake. A light squeak is often dust or glazed linings. A grinding or scraping sound usually means the lining has worn through to the metal shoe, a lining has come loose, or there is grit or a broken spring inside the drum. A noise with the brake off may be a dragging shoe or a wheel bearing.

Is it safe to ride?

Ride with care to a place to work on it if it only squeaks and still brakes well. If it grinds or scrapes, or braking is weaker, do not ride.

Quick checks first

  • Does the noise happen only when braking, or all the time?
  • Look at the wear indicator pointer with the brake on.
  • Spin the wheel on the stand: does it rub at one point?
  • Has the bike been through mud or sand?
  • A grooved or glazed drum can be sanded smooth. Turning it on a lathe is better but costs more. Do not breathe the dust.
  • Grease the cam shaft and pivot post lightly when the shoes are out.

Diagnose it step by step

1Is there noise with the brake fully released?

  • Yes: go to step 2.
  • No, only when braking: go to step 4.

2Back off the brake adjuster several turns. Does the noise stop?

  • Yes: the brake was set too tight or the cam does not return. Adjust a drum brake; if the arm is slow to return, Lubricate control cables and check the cam during Service a drum brake.
  • No: go to step 3.

3Does the wheel feel rough or move sideways on the axle?

  • Yes: the noise is a wheel bearing. Check wheel bearings and Rumbling or grinding from a wheel.
  • No: open the drum: a broken spring or loose lining may be touching. Service a drum brake.

4Is the wear pointer past the end of its mark?

  • Yes: shoes worn to the metal. Do not ride. Service a drum brake with new shoes, and check the drum for deep scoring.
  • No: go to step 5.

5Is the noise a high squeak rather than a grind?

  • Yes: open the drum, remove the dust with a damp cloth (never blow it), de-glaze shoes and drum lightly with fine abrasive paper. Service a drum brake.
  • No, it scrapes: a lining is loose or cracked, or grit is inside. Open the drum and replace damaged shoes.

6After service, does a squeak remain?

  • Check the backing plate is not bent and the torque arm is tight. Fit shoes from a different brand listed for your bike.

Never put oil or grease on the outside of a squeaking hub to quieten it: it can find its way to the linings.

Causes, most likely first

Cause How to confirm Fix
Dust inside drum Squeak; dusty when opened Clean with damp cloth
Glazed shoes or drum Shiny surfaces De-glaze or replace shoes
Shoes worn to metal Pointer past mark; grinding New shoes; check drum
Over-adjusted or sticking cam Noise with brake off; stops when backed off Adjust a drum brake
Broken spring or loose lining Scraping; seen when opened Service a drum brake
Wheel bearing Rough or loose wheel Check wheel bearings
