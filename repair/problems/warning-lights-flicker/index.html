Lights, horn and instruments

Warning lights flicker or come on wrongly

Ride with careAlso called: dash lights flicker, random warning lamps, ghost warnings, neutral light glows in gear

Warning lamps on the dashboard flash, glow faintly, or come on when nothing seems wrong. Several lamps flickering together usually means low or unsteady voltage, or a poor earth shared by the dashboard and the sensors. One lamp acting up usually means a fault in that lamp's sensor, switch or wire. On fuel-injected and CAN-bus bikes, a loose earth can even make the ECU store fault codes that do not match any real fault: the K-Light manual names this exact case (PDF p. 276).

Is it safe to ride?

First decide whether the warning could be real. If the oil pressure lamp or the temperature lamp comes on while riding, stop as soon as it is safe and turn the engine off: see Oil pressure or oil level light is on and Temperature gauge reads high or the warning light is on. If the ABS lamp stays on, ABS may not work: brake gently. Otherwise, ride with care to get it checked.

Quick checks first

  • Which lamps flicker: one, or several at once?
  • Does it happen at idle, when you rev, over bumps, or after rain?
  • Is the battery old, or are the terminals loose?
  • Has anything been fitted recently: LED bulbs, an alarm, a USB charger?
  • Lamps that glow very faintly with the key off can be normal on some LED dashboards fed by a leak from another circuit, but check for added accessories.

Diagnose it step by step

1Do several lamps flicker together?

  • Yes: go to step 2.
  • Only one lamp: go to step 6.

2. Is the battery voltage good and steady? Set the multimeter to DC volts (V with a straight line), 20 V range. Red probe on battery positive, black probe on battery negative. Read it with the engine off, then running with revs raised.

  • Low with engine off: Test and charge a 12 V battery.
  • Jumps up and down, or goes too high with revs (K-Light 125 should be 14.5 ± 0.5 V at 5000 rpm, PDF p. 78): Test the charging system and see Battery overcharges and bulbs blow.
  • Good and steady: go to step 3.

3. Are the battery terminals and main earths tight and clean? Wiggle each terminal while watching the dashboard.

  • Flicker follows the wiggle: Clean and tighten battery terminals and Clean and tighten earth connections. On a fuel-injected bike, the K-Light manual then says to disconnect the battery for 3 minutes before restarting (PDF p. 276).
  • No change: go to step 4.

4. Is the dashboard supply good? Follow Check power to the instruments: test the feed and earth at the dashboard plug, and look inside it for water.

  • Poor earth or wet plug: Clean and seal electrical connectors.
  • Good: go to step 5.

5. Does the flicker come with bumps or steering? The harness may be broken at the steering head or chafed: Find a wiring fault.

6One lamp only. Which one?

  • Engine (FI or MIL) lamp: on the K-Light 125 it lights at key on and goes out after starting (PDF p. 269). If it comes on while riding, read the fault code: Read engine fault codes (fuel injection) and see Engine warning light (check engine, MIL) is on or flashing.
  • Neutral lamp glowing in gear or flickering: see Neutral light does not work or stays on in gear.
  • ABS lamp: see ABS warning light is on.
  • Oil or temperature lamp: treat as real until proved otherwise: links above.
  • Indicator lamp flashing fast: see Indicators flash too fast.
  • Bulb or lamp warning on a CAN-bus bike: a non-approved LED, a poor lamp earth or a loose holder can set it, as HELLA's workshop guide explains (see sources). Check those, then see a dealer.
