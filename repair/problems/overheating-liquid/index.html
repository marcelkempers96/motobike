Cooling

Liquid-cooled engine overheats

Stop nowAlso called: boiling over, coolant boiling, temperature in the red, steam from radiator

A liquid-cooled engine pumps coolant through the engine and a radiator. A thermostat holds the coolant in the engine until it is warm, and an electric fan pulls air through the radiator in slow traffic. Overheating shows as the temperature gauge in the red, a warning light, coolant boiling out of the reserve tank, steam, or a sweet smell. Causes, from simple to serious: low coolant, a fan that does not switch on, a blocked or damaged radiator, a stuck thermostat, a failed water pump, and a leaking head gasket.

Is it safe to ride?

Stop now, switch off, and let it cool fully. Do not open the radiator cap while hot: boiling coolant can spray out and scald you.

Quick checks first

  • Stop and switch off. Wait until the engine is cold.
  • Was the fan running when you stopped in traffic? (You can hear and feel it.)
  • Reserve tank level once cold.
  • Any wet spots or steam at hoses or the radiator?
  • Open only the pressure cap on the radiator, never the reservoir cap, and only when the engine is cold. Look for the pump flow in the filler neck.

Diagnose it step by step

1Once cold, is the reserve tank below the LOWER mark or empty? Check with Check and top up coolant.

  • Yes: top up. Then find why it was low: Coolant is leaking. If the radiator itself was empty, a workshop should refill and bleed it.
  • No: go to step 2.

2Did it overheat mainly in slow traffic, and was the fan silent?

  • Yes: go to Radiator fan does not switch on and Test the radiator fan and its switch.
  • No, it also overheats when moving: go to step 3.

3Is the radiator front blocked with mud, insects, plastic or bent fins?

  • Yes: clean gently with low-pressure water from behind. See Radiator is damaged, bent or blocked.
  • No: go to step 4.

4Is the gauge reading high while the radiator and upper hose stay cool?

  • Yes: coolant is not reaching the radiator: thermostat stuck closed, water pump failure or an air lock. Ask for Check the thermostat. If the coolant was just changed, bleed air with Replace the coolant.
  • No, the radiator gets hot too: go to step 5.

5Is there white smoke, milky oil or bubbles in the reserve tank while running?

  • Yes: head gasket or cracked head. Go to White smoke or steam from the exhaust. Ask for Do a leak-down test.
  • No: go to step 6.

6Is the gauge reading high but the engine is not really hot (no boiling, normal fan action)?

  • Yes: the sensor or gauge may be wrong. Go to Temperature gauge reads high or the warning light is on.
  • No: old coolant can scale up the radiator. Change it with Replace the coolant and have a workshop check the water pump.

Causes, most likely first

Cause How to confirm Fix
Low coolant Reserve tank level Check and top up coolant
Fan not working Silent in traffic Test the radiator fan and its switch
Blocked radiator Mud, bent fins Find a coolant leak
Thermostat stuck closed Hot engine, cool radiator Check the thermostat
Air lock after refill Recent coolant work Replace the coolant
Head gasket Bubbles, white smoke Do a leak-down test
Thermostat not opening Suspend it in a pan of heating water. It should open at the temperature marked on it Check the thermostat. Do not run without it to cure heat
