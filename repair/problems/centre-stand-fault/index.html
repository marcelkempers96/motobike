Frame, stands and bodywork

Centre stand is hard to use or scrapes

OK to rideAlso called: main stand hard to lift, centre stand drags in corners, main stand rattles

Getting the bike onto the centre stand (main stand) takes a lot of effort, the bike rocks on the stand, or the folded stand scrapes the road when cornering. Often the cause is technique, a dry pivot, weak springs or a sagging rear suspension.

Is it safe to ride?

Yes, if the stand stays folded up. If it scrapes the road in corners or hangs down, fix it before riding: a stand that digs in can lift the rear wheel.

Quick checks first

  • Are you using your foot on the stand tab and lifting by the frame or grab rail, not the seat?
  • Fold the stand up: does it snap tight against its stop?
  • Is the rubber stop pad there?
  • Does the rear sit lower than normal, especially with a load?

Diagnose it step by step

1Is it hard to lift the bike onto the stand?

  • Yes: go to step 2.
  • No: go to step 4.

2Are you pressing down on the stand tab with your full weight on that foot, and pulling up and back by a solid frame part?

  • No: use the technique on Check the centre stand. Most effort problems disappear.
  • Yes: go to step 3.

3Is the stand pivot dry, rusty or tight?

  • Yes: remove, clean and grease the pivot (Check the centre stand).
  • No: check for bent legs or a rear tyre much larger than standard, which make lifting harder.

4Does the bike rock or lean when on the stand?

  • Yes: the legs are bent or the feet worn. Replace the stand. Check the brackets for cracks.
  • No: go to step 5.

5Does the folded stand scrape the road in corners or over bumps?

  • Yes: go to step 6.
  • No: go to step 7.

6Does the stand fold fully up and is the spring strong?

  • No: fit new springs and a stop pad.
  • Yes: the rear may be sitting too low. See Rear sits too low, Adjust rear spring preload, Check the rear shock absorbers.

7Does the stand rattle when riding?

  • Yes: worn stop pad, weak spring or loose pivot. Fix as in step 6, and see Rattling from panels, mudguards or the seat.

Causes, most likely first

Cause How to confirm Fix
Wrong technique Easy when done correctly Use foot on tab, lift by frame
Dry or rusty pivot Stiff movement Clean and grease
Weak springs, missing stop pad Stand not tight when folded New springs and pad
Bent legs Bike rocks on stand New stand
Rear suspension sagging Scrapes with load Rear sits too low
