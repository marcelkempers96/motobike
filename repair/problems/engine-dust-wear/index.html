Engine noise and internals

Engine wears out early after dirt gets past the air filter

Ride with careAlso called: dusted engine, dirt in the engine, engine worn by dust, sucked dirt

The engine starts to use oil, loses power and becomes harder to start long before it should, often after riding on dusty roads, after a filter change, or after work on the intake. Dust that gets past the air filter acts like grinding paste. It wears the piston rings and bore, the valve stems and guides, the throttle slide and shaft, and, once it reaches the oil, the crank bearings. A dusted engine wears many times faster than a clean one, so find the leak quickly.

Is it safe to ride?

Yes, with care, for a short distance to a workshop. Do not keep riding with a torn or missing filter: every kilometre adds wear.

Quick checks first

  • Open the airbox. Is the filter there, whole and seated in its groove?
  • Look at the clean side of the filter and inside the intake to the carburettor or throttle body. Is there dust there?
  • Is the intake rubber between the airbox and the carburettor split or loose?
  • Is the oil level falling between changes? Is there blue smoke?

Diagnose it step by step

1Is there dust on the clean side of the filter or inside the intake?

  • Yes: dirt is getting in. Go to step 2.
  • No: go to step 3.

2Is the filter torn, the wrong size, not seated, or is the airbox lid or intake rubber leaking?

  • Yes: fit the correct filter, seat it properly and replace any split rubber or clip: Clean or replace the air filter, Check the airbox inlet, filter and breather for a blockage. Then go to step 3 to judge the wear already done.
  • No leak found, but still dusty: check the airbox for cracks and the breather and drain pipes.

3Does the engine use oil, smoke blue, or feel weak?

  • Yes: test compression: Compression test. Go to step 4.
  • No: the filter fix may have been in time. Keep checking the filter more often in dusty conditions.

4Is compression below the value in your bike's manual?

  • Yes: the rings and bore are worn. Plan a top-end job: Assess the top end.
  • No: keep riding, check the oil level often, and recheck compression later.

5During the strip-down, are there fine vertical scratches all round the bore and a dull, scratched piston skirt?

  • Yes: dirt wear confirmed. Measure: Measure piston-to-cylinder clearance and bore wear. Check valve stems and guides too, and the crank bearings if the oil was gritty.
  • No: the cause may be something else. See Read a failed engine to find the cause.

What happens if you rebuild without fixing the leak: the new rings and bore wear out again in a short time, and the engine smokes after the rebuild.

Causes, most likely first

Cause How to confirm Fix
Filter torn, missing or not seated Dust on the clean side Clean or replace the air filter
Foam filter dry (not oiled) Foam dry to touch, dust passed through Clean and re-oil a foam air filter
Split intake rubber or loose clip Crack or gap between airbox and carburettor Replace the rubber or clip
Filter left dirty too long in dust Filter blocked; dust found round its edge Service the filter more often
Wrong filter fitted Gap round the edge of the filter Fit the correct part
