Smoke and exhaust

Black smoke from the exhaust

Ride with careAlso called: sooty exhaust, running rich, black soot at the tailpipe

Black smoke is unburned fuel. The engine is getting too much fuel for the air it breathes (a rich mixture). Common causes are a blocked air filter, a choke left on or stuck, a carburettor float that lets fuel overflow, or on fuel-injected bikes a leaking injector or a faulty sensor. A rich engine wastes fuel, fouls spark plugs, smells of petrol, and over time can thin the engine oil and damage a catalytic converter.

Is it safe to ride?

Yes with care for a short distance. Stop if fuel is dripping, the oil level is rising, or the engine warning light is flashing.

Quick checks first

  • Is the choke lever or knob fully off?
  • When was the air filter last cleaned or changed?
  • Is fuel dripping from the carburettor overflow pipe?
  • Is the engine warning light (MIL) on, on a fuel-injected bike?

Diagnose it step by step

1Confirm the colour with Diagnose exhaust smoke by colour. Does a white card held near the outlet get dry black soot?

  • Yes, dry soot: rich mixture. Go to step 2.
  • Oily wet spots: this is oil. Go to Blue or grey smoke from the exhaust.

2Is the choke fully off, and does the engine still smoke when warm?

  • Choke on or stuck: free it with Check the choke or starter valve. See Choke is stuck on or will not work.
  • Choke off: go to step 3.

3Remove and look at the air filter with Clean or replace the air filter. Is it dirty, oily or wet?

  • Yes: clean or replace it. Never ride with the filter removed. Retest.
  • No: go to step 4.

4Carburettor bike: is fuel overflowing from the overflow pipe, or does the engine flood when parked?

  • Yes: go to Fuel pours out of the carburettor overflow pipe and Check the carburettor float and needle valve.
  • No: the jets or air passages may be worn or wrong. Do Clean the carburettor. Do not guess jet sizes.

5Fuel-injected bike: is the warning light on, or are there stored codes? Read them with Read engine fault codes (fuel injection).

  • Code for oxygen sensor, temperature sensor or MAP sensor: go to Check the engine sensors (TPS, MAP, temperature).
  • No code: the K-Light manual notes that an oxygen sensor reading high when fully warm points to a leaking injector. Go to Check the fuel injector.

6. Read the plug with Read a spark plug. Black and dry confirms rich running. A weak spark can also leave fuel unburned; see Spark plug is black and sooty.

7. Check the oil for a petrol smell. If it smells of fuel, see Oil smells of petrol or the level rises and change the oil after the fault is fixed.

Causes, most likely first

Cause How to confirm Fix
Choke left on or stuck Lever position, smoke when warm Check the choke or starter valve
Blocked air filter Dirty element Clean or replace the air filter
Carburettor float or needle valve Overflow, flooding Check the carburettor float and needle valve
Dirty or worn carburettor Black plug, no overflow Clean the carburettor
EFI sensor fault Fault code Check the engine sensors (TPS, MAP, temperature)
Leaking injector Rich with no code Check the fuel injector
