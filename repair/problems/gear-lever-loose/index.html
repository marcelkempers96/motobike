Gearbox and gear change

Gear lever is loose, bent or slipping on its shaft

Ride with careAlso called: gear pedal loose, shifter slipping, bent gear lever

The gear lever moves before the gears do, flops around, sits at a strange angle or is bent after a fall. A loose clamp bolt or worn splines (the fine teeth that lock the lever to the shaft) let it slip, so changes are incomplete and the bike finds false neutrals. If it comes off, you cannot change gear at all. Sometimes the shaft itself moves in the engine because its bush or seal is worn.

Is it safe to ride?

Ride with care and fix it soon. Carry the right spanner. If the lever falls off you will be stuck in one gear.

Quick checks first

  • Is the clamp bolt present and tight?
  • Is the lever bent or cracked?
  • Does the shaft (spindle) wobble in the engine, or is oil weeping around it?
  • Was the bike dropped on this side?

Diagnose it step by step

1Is the clamp bolt loose or missing?

  • Yes: refit and tighten it fully through the groove in the shaft. Then go to step 2 to check the splines.
  • No: go to step 2.

2With the bolt tight, can you move the lever without the shaft turning?

  • Yes: the splines are worn. Remove the lever (Refit or replace the gear lever) and look at both sets of teeth. Lever teeth worn: new lever. Shaft teeth worn: workshop, Assess internal gearbox faults.
  • No: go to step 3.

3Is the lever bent, cracked, or set so it touches the case or footpeg?

  • Yes: replace a cracked or badly bent lever; do not heat or hammer a cast lever straight. Refit or replace the gear lever.
  • No: go to step 4.

4On a linkage type, are the ball joints or the pedal pivot loose?

  • Yes: Check the gear lever and shift linkage, Service footpegs and foot pedals.
  • No: go to step 5.

5Does the shaft itself wobble up and down, or is oil leaking around it?

  • Yes: the bush or seal is worn. Trace an oil leak to confirm; seal replacement is a workshop job.
  • No: the lever is fixed.

6Was the bike crashed or dropped?

  • Yes: check the footpeg, brake pedal, side stand and frame as well: The bike has been dropped or crashed. A hard hit on the lever can also bend the shaft inside the engine.
