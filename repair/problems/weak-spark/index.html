Ignition

Weak, thin or orange spark

Ride with careAlso called: weak spark, yellow spark, thin spark, poor spark

The spark tester shows a spark, but it is thin, orange or red instead of bright blue-white, or it misses some turns. A weak spark may start the engine when conditions are easy but fail under load, at high revs, in rain or when cold. Colour is only a rough guide: a spark that looks fine in open air can still fail under compression. The K-Light 125 fault chart lists coil supply, ignition switch, CDI or ECU, trigger coil, battery charge and low-voltage wiring (PDF page 21).

Is it safe to ride?

With care. A weak spark can cause misfires and sudden loss of power. Avoid busy roads until it is fixed.

Quick checks first

  • Is the battery fully charged? A weak battery gives a weak spark on battery-powered systems.
  • Is the plug cap tight on the plug and lead, and dry inside?
  • Is the coil firmly mounted to clean metal?
  • How old is the plug?

Diagnose it step by step

1Is the battery charged, and does its voltage stay up while cranking?

  • No: Test and charge a 12 V battery.
  • Yes, or magneto bike: go to step 2.

2With a new, correctly gapped plug, is the spark now strong?

  • Yes: the old plug was worn or fouled. Remove, check, gap and fit a spark plug. Find why it fouled: Read a spark plug.
  • No: go to step 3.

3Are the plug cap and lead free of cracks, burn tracks and corrosion, and does the cap test good?

  • No: replace the cap, or the coil if the lead is part of it. Test the ignition coil and plug cap.
  • Yes: go to step 4.

4Are the coil earth, the main earth and the coil supply connectors clean and tight?

  • No: Clean and tighten earth connections and Clean and seal electrical connectors.
  • Yes: go to step 5.

5Does a known good coil give a strong spark?

  • Yes: the old coil is weak. Replace it.
  • No: go to step 6.

6Does the pick-up pass its resistance and peak voltage tests? (K-Light 125: 150 ± 10 Ω at 20 °C; peak above 1.7 V)

  • No: Test the ignition pick-up.
  • Yes: go to step 7.

7Magneto CDI: does the source coil in the stator test good?

  • No: Test the stator (generator coils).
  • Yes, or battery-powered: Diagnose a suspected CDI or ECU fault.

Causes, most likely first

Cause How to confirm Fix
Worn or fouled plug New plug gives a strong spark Remove, check, gap and fit a spark plug
Low battery voltage Voltage drops a lot when cranking Test and charge a 12 V battery
Cracked or wet plug cap or lead Tracking marks; cap test Test the ignition coil and plug cap
Poor coil earth or connector Corrosion; loose mount Clean and tighten earth connections
Weak coil Swap test Test the ignition coil and plug cap
Weak pick-up or source coil Low peak voltage Test the ignition pick-up
CDI or ECU All else good Diagnose a suspected CDI or ECU fault
Worn points or a failed condenser (points ignition) Pitted faces, wrong gap or heavy arcing Inspect, clean and replace contact breaker points
Pick-up air gap too wide (electronic ignition) Gap to the flywheel magnet checked with a non-magnetic gauge; use the value in your bike's manual Test an ignition pick-up
