Fuel system

Fuel pump whines loudly or runs all the time

Ride with careAlso called: noisy fuel pump, pump keeps running, pump whine

The in-tank pump normally makes a short, quiet buzz at key-on and a faint hum while running. A loud whine, a rising or rattling note, or a pump that keeps running after the priming seconds with the engine off, means something is wrong. A pump that works hard is often starving: the tank is low or its strainer is blocked. A pump that never stops is being kept on by a stuck relay or a wiring or ECU fault, and it will flatten the battery.

Is it safe to ride?

With care, for short distances, and keep the tank well filled. A starving pump can fail and stop the engine without warning.

Quick checks first

  • How much fuel is in the tank? Pumps get louder and hotter on a low tank, because fuel cools them.
  • Is the noise only at key-on, or all the time the engine runs?
  • With the key on and the engine off, does the pump stop after a few seconds?
  • Has the bike had dirty or rusty fuel?

Diagnose it step by step

1Fill the tank to at least half. Is the noise now normal?

  • Yes: the pump was running near-dry. Do not run the tank low. The K-Light 125 manual warns that running the pump with little or no fuel can burn it out.
  • No: go to step 2.

2Key ON, engine off. Does the pump stop after its priming seconds (K-Light 125: about 3 s)?

  • No, it keeps running: the pump is being held on. A stuck relay or a wiring or ECU fault. Turn the key off. Check fuses and relays (swap the relay with an identical one), then read fault codes (K-Light 125: P0629 pump circuit shorted). See Find a wiring fault. Check the battery has not been drained: Test and charge a 12 V battery.
  • Yes, it stops normally: go to step 3.

3Has the bike had dirty, rusty or watery fuel, or a long time since the fuel filter was changed?

  • Yes: the pump strainer or filter is probably blocked and the pump is straining. Drain the fuel and ask a workshop to clean or replace the in-tank strainer and filter. See Rust or flakes inside the fuel tank.
  • No: go to step 4.

4Does the engine also lose power, hesitate, or cut out?

  • Yes: the pump may be wearing out, or a pressure fault. A workshop measures fuel pressure (K-Light 125: about 300 kPa; below 270 kPa is a fault). The K-Light 125 manual also notes that a reversed pump connector makes the pump run backwards with no pressure. Check the fuel pump.
  • No, it runs well but is noisy: monitor it. Note whether the noise grows. Replace the pump assembly when it starts to affect running.
