Oil and leaks

Oil turns black very soon after a change

OK to rideAlso called: black oil, dirty oil, oil goes dark fast

Modern oil carries soot and dirt in suspension so they drain out at the next change. Some darkening within a few hundred kilometres is normal, especially on an older engine. Oil that is black within a day or two, gritty, or sludgy points to blow-by (combustion gas leaking past rings), dust getting in through a damaged air filter or intake, a dirty engine that was not flushed, or a centrifugal filter that is packed full. Old sludge mixed into new oil also makes it dark fast.

Is it safe to ride?

Yes. Plan the checks below and change oil more often until the cause is found.

Quick checks first

  • Was the strainer cleaned at the last change?
  • Is the air filter in good condition and sealed in its box?
  • Any blue smoke or smoke from the breather?
  • Is the oil black and smooth, or gritty?

Diagnose it step by step

1Look at the oil and strainer with Inspect drained oil for clues. Is it gritty or sandy to the touch?

  • Yes: dust is getting into the engine. Go to step 2.
  • No, smooth and black: go to step 3.
  • Metal flakes: go to Metal particles or flakes in the oil.

2Check the air filter and intake with Clean or replace the air filter and Find an intake air leak. Is the filter torn, badly fitted or the intake rubber split?

  • Yes: fix it now. Dust entering the engine wears rings and the cylinder quickly.
  • No: go to step 3.

3Was the engine full of old sludge when you started changing oil more often?

  • Yes: change the oil and clean the strainer with Change the engine oil and clean the strainer twice at a short interval. Many engines also have a centrifugal filter that a workshop can clean.
  • No: go to step 4.

4Is there smoke from the breather or oil filler at idle? Check with Check the crankcase breather.

  • Yes: blow-by. Go to step 5.
  • No: some darkening is normal. Keep to the service interval in the manual (K-Light 125: every 3000 km) or shorter in dusty use.

5Do Test engine compression. Low, or rising with the wet test?

  • Yes: worn rings. See Low compression.
  • No: rings may be partly stuck. Shorter oil intervals often help.

Also check: a rich mixture (black smoke) adds soot to the oil. See Black smoke from the exhaust.

Causes, most likely first

Cause How to confirm Fix
Old sludge in a neglected engine Black from the first day Change the engine oil and clean the strainer
Dust entry (filter or intake) Gritty oil Clean or replace the air filter
Blow-by from worn rings Breather smoke, low compression Test engine compression
Rich running Black smoke, sooty plug Black smoke from the exhaust
