Brakes

Rear brake pedal travels too far

Do not rideAlso called: rear brake pedal low, pedal goes to the floor, have to press the rear brake hard and far

You have to push the rear brake pedal a long way down before the brake works, or it reaches the footpeg or its stop. On a drum brake this is usually adjustment or worn shoes. On a hydraulic (disc) rear brake it suggests air, a low fluid level, a worn master cylinder, or the pedal and pushrod set wrongly.

Is it safe to ride?

No, until the pedal works within its normal travel. This is usually a quick fix.

Quick checks first

  • Measure the free play at the pedal pad (K-Light 125: 20 to 30 mm).
  • Drum brake: look at the wear indicator pointer.
  • Hydraulic brake: check the rear reservoir (and CBS reservoir) level.
  • Has the chain or rear wheel just been adjusted?

Diagnose it step by step

1Is the rear brake a drum (rod or cable) or a disc (hydraulic)?

  • Drum: go to step 2.
  • Disc: go to step 4.

2Drum: does turning the adjuster in bring the free play back to the manual value?

  • Yes: Adjust a drum brake. Check the brake light after adjusting.
  • No, the adjuster is at its end: go to step 3.

3Is the wear pointer past its mark, or the brake arm past a right angle when applied?

  • Yes: worn shoes or drum. Service a drum brake.
  • No: the cam or arm may have been refitted on the wrong spline. Check during the service.

4Disc: is the fluid level above LOWER in the rear (and CBS) reservoir?

  • No: check pads with Check brake pad wear and look for leaks. See Brake fluid is leaking.
  • Yes: go to step 5.

5Is the pedal free play too large because the pushrod or stop bolt is set wrong?

  • Yes: Adjust the rear brake pedal. On the K-Light 125, the pushrod should leave 0 to 0.2 mm gap at the piston and the pedal free play should be 20 to 30 mm.
  • No: go to step 6.

6Does the pedal feel soft, or sink when held?

  • Soft: air. Replace brake fluid and bleed the brakes; on a CBS bike use Check a combined braking system (CBS).
  • Sinks: Check the brake master cylinder, and on CBS bikes the CBS internal leak test.

7Is the pedal bent, or its pivot worn so it moves sideways?

  • Yes: see Foot pedal or footpeg is stiff, bent or loose.

Causes, most likely first

Cause How to confirm Fix
Drum brake needs adjustment Large free play; adjuster has thread left Adjust a drum brake
Worn drum shoes Pointer past mark Service a drum brake
Pedal or pushrod set wrong Free play outside manual range Adjust the rear brake pedal
Air or low fluid (disc) Soft pedal; low reservoir Replace brake fluid and bleed the brakes
Worn master cylinder or CBS leak Pedal sinks when held Check the brake master cylinder
