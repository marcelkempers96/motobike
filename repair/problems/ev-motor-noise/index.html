Electric drive

Grinding, clicking or growling from the motor

Ride with careAlso called: hub motor noise, motor whine, clicking wheel, rumbling motor

A new noise comes from the motor area: grinding, rumbling, clicking, scraping or a growl. Electric motors are normally quiet, with a soft whine under power. The key question is whether the noise follows wheel speed (heard even when coasting with the power off) or motor power (only when you open the throttle). Noise that follows wheel speed is usually mechanical: bearings, brake, chain or something rubbing. Noise only under power is often electrical: a Hall sensor or phase fault makes the motor growl (em3ev).

Is it safe to ride?

Ride with care to the workshop if the noise is mild. Stop riding if you hear loud grinding, the wheel wobbles, or you smell burning.

Quick checks first

  • Coast with the throttle closed: does the noise continue?
  • Is anything caught in the wheel (string, wire, plastic bag)?
  • Is the brake dragging? Is the wheel hot near the brake?
  • On a chain bike: is the chain dry or slack?

Diagnose it step by step

1Do you still hear the noise when coasting with the throttle closed (or pushing the bike with power off)?

  • Yes: mechanical. Go to step 2.
  • No, only under power: go to step 5.

2. Is something rubbing: brake, mudguard, tyre, debris? Spin the wheel by hand on the stand.

  • Yes: remove debris; for brakes see Check for brake drag.
  • No: go to step 3.

3. Is there play when you rock the wheel, or a rumble that rises with speed? Check hub motor bearings and play.

  • Yes: worn bearings (Letrigo). Hub motor bearings are a workshop job. Compare Rumbling or grinding from a wheel.
  • No: go to step 4.

4On a chain or belt drive, is the chain dry, rusty, slack or the sprocket worn?

  • Yes: see Chain is noisy, clattering or slapping.
  • No: workshop. A geared hub motor can have worn internal gears or clutch; this needs opening by the dealer.

5Does the motor growl or stutter mainly when starting from a stop?

  • Yes: Hall sensor or phase fault (em3ev). Go to Bike judders or jerks when pulling away.
  • No: go to step 6.

6Is it a high whine or buzzing that is new or much louder than before?

  • Yes: can be a controller setting, a failing controller or a motor winding fault. Read codes: Read electric drive error codes, then workshop: Diagnose a motor fault.

What happens if you keep riding with bad bearings: the motor shell can rub on the stator and destroy the motor, which costs far more than bearings.

Causes, most likely first

Cause How to confirm Fix
Debris or rubbing part Visible when spinning wheel Remove it
Brake dragging Wheel slows quickly, brake warm Check for brake drag
Worn wheel or motor bearings Rumble with speed; play Check hub motor bearings and play
Chain or sprocket wear Noise from drive side Check the chain for wear
Hall or phase fault Growl under power at start Check motor position (Hall) sensors
Internal motor damage All above good Diagnose a motor fault
