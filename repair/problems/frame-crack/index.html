Frame, stands and bodywork

Crack in the frame or a bracket

Do not rideAlso called: cracked frame, broken weld, cracked subframe, cracked footpeg bracket

You see a crack in a frame tube, at a weld, at the steering head, at an engine mount, in the rear subframe or in a bracket for a footpeg, stand, carrier or seat. Cracks start from overloading (heavy cargo, passengers), crashes, potholes, vibration from loose engine mounts, or rust. A crack grows with every ride and can break suddenly.

Is it safe to ride?

No, if the crack is in the main frame, steering head, swingarm, engine mounts or subframe. A small crack in a non-structural bracket (for example a panel tab) is less urgent, but have it checked.

Quick checks first

  • Clean the area and look with a torch. Paint that is cracked in a line, rust streaks or a black line at a weld can hide a crack.
  • Is anything bolted to the cracked part loose?
  • Has the bike carried very heavy loads, or been in a crash?

Diagnose it step by step

1Where is the crack?

  • Main frame, steering head, swingarm, engine mount, subframe: do not ride. Go to step 4.
  • Bracket for a footpeg, stand, seat, carrier or panel: go to step 2.

2Is the part held by the bracket loose, or has it been loose for some time?

  • Yes: a loose part hammers its bracket. After repair, tighten it to the correct value and check it often (Check chassis fasteners).
  • No: go to step 3.

3Is the crack only in paint, or in the metal? Clean to bare metal or ask a workshop to dye-test it.

  • Paint only: touch up and watch it.
  • Metal: the bracket must be replaced or repaired by a competent welder. Remove the fuel tank and battery before any welding nearby.

4Was the bike in a crash or hit something hard?

  • Yes: the frame may also be bent. See The bike has been dropped or crashed and Inspect the frame after a crash.
  • No: go to step 5.

5Are the engine mounts tight, and has the bike carried heavy loads?

  • Loose mounts: Check engine mounting bolts. Loose mounts let the engine shake the frame.
  • Heavy loads: keep loads within the maker's limit, and use a carrier designed for the bike.

6Workshop decision:

  • A frame specialist decides if a steel frame can be repaired, and checks alignment after. Aluminium frames are usually replaced. Do not ride until it is repaired and checked.

Causes, most likely first

Cause How to confirm Fix
Loose part hammering a bracket Bolts loose, crack at mount New bracket, tighten
Overloading Crack at subframe or carrier mounts Workshop repair, reduce load
Crash or pothole Crack plus bent parts Crash inspection
Loose engine mounts Mount bolts loose, vibration Engine mounts
Rust Crack in rusted area Workshop repair or new frame
