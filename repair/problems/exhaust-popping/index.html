Smoke and exhaust

Popping or banging from the exhaust when slowing down

Ride with careAlso called: backfire on deceleration, afterfire, crackling exhaust, popping on overrun

When you close the throttle, the engine gets very little fuel. If extra air gets into the exhaust (through a leaking joint or a loose silencer), or if the mixture is too lean, unburned fuel can light in the hot pipe and pop or bang. A few soft pops on a bike with a free-flowing aftermarket exhaust are common. Loud bangs, or new popping after work on the exhaust or carburettor, point to a leak or a mixture fault. Some bikes have an air injection system that adds air to the exhaust for emissions; a fault in it can also cause popping.

Is it safe to ride?

Yes with care. Get it checked; a lean engine runs hot and loud bangs can damage the silencer.

Quick checks first

  • Is there a ticking or puffing at the exhaust joint near the head?
  • Has the exhaust or silencer been changed, or the silencer baffle removed?
  • Has the air filter, carburettor or intake rubber been worked on?
  • Fuel-injected bike: is the warning light on?

Diagnose it step by step

1With the engine cold, look and listen at the exhaust joints. Any soot streaks, ticking or a loose silencer clamp?

  • Yes: fix it with Find and fix an exhaust leak. This is the most common cause.
  • No: go to step 2.

2Has the exhaust been replaced with an open or louder one?

  • Yes: some popping is normal with less restriction. A fuel-injected bike may need its fuel map matched by the dealer. On a carburettor bike the mixture may need checking. Go to step 4.
  • No: go to step 3.

3Is there an intake air leak: cracked intake rubber, loose clamp, split vacuum hose? Check with Find an intake air leak.

  • Yes: fix it. An air leak makes the mixture lean.
  • No: go to step 4.

4Carburettor bike: is the idle mixture or idle speed set wrongly? Check with Adjust carburettor idle speed and mixture.

  • Fuel-injected: read codes with Read engine fault codes (fuel injection). Go to step 5.

5Four-stroke: is the valve clearance correct? Too little clearance can hold the exhaust valve slightly open. Check with Check and adjust valve clearance.

  • Out of range: set it.
  • Correct: go to step 6.

6Does it also pop when starting or under load?

  • Yes: ignition timing or a loose pick-up may be involved. The K-Light manual links backfire to a loose coil or reluctor ring. Ask for Check the ignition timing. See Pops or bangs when trying to start.
  • No: see Engine runs hot and lean, pops and pinks.
