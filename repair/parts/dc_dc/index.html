Electric drive and battery

DC-DC converter

Find this part →See it in 3D

The DC-DC converter is a small sealed box, often with fins, near the controller or battery. It takes power from the high-voltage traction battery and turns it down to about 12 V.

What it does

An electric bike has no alternator. The DC-DC converter does that job: it powers the lights, horn, dashboard and control electronics and keeps the 12 V auxiliary battery charged while the bike is on. In good designs the high-voltage side is isolated from the 12 V side inside the box. If it fails, the bike runs on the 12 V battery alone until that goes flat, and then lights, dash and the ready system stop.

Signs it is worn or failing

  • Small battery keeps going flat: Small 12 V battery is flat.
  • Lights dim, dashboard resets or warnings flicker while riding.
  • Bike dies and will not go to ready: Will not go to ready, Dashboard is dark.
  • These signs can look like a traction battery fault; test before replacing parts.

When to replace it

After testing confirms the 12 V output is missing or wrong with the bike on and the inputs and fuses are good. Check its fuse and connectors first.

What to match when buying

  • Exact part for your bike: input voltage range, output voltage and current, connectors and mounting.
  • A generic converter may not be isolated or may not suit the pack voltage. Buy through the maker or dealer.

Fitting notes

  • Its input side is high voltage: Make an electric motorcycle safe to work on.
  • Test: Check the 12 V auxiliary system.
