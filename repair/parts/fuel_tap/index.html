Fuel tap

Also called: petcock, fuel valve

Find this part →See it in 3D

The fuel tap is a small valve under the tank of a carburettor bike, with a lever marked OFF, ON and RES (reserve). A vacuum tap has a small hose to the engine intake and opens only when the engine runs; some have a PRI (prime) position. Fuel-injected bikes have no tap; they use an in-tank fuel pump.

What it does

The tap lets fuel flow to the carburettor and stops it when the bike is parked. Two pickup tubes give a main supply and a lower reserve, so when the bike starts to stutter on ON, turning to RES gives a little more fuel. A strainer on the pickup and a sediment bowl catch dirt. Seals harden, strainers block, and vacuum diaphragms split.

Signs it is worn or failing

  • No fuel reaching the carburettor.
  • Engine stops and only runs on reserve or after a rest.
  • Fuel leaking at the lever or tank joint.
  • Carb overflowing when parked on a vacuum tap that no longer shuts.

When to replace it

When it leaks, will not shut, or still flows badly after the strainer is cleaned. Vacuum taps can sometimes be rebuilt with a diaphragm kit.

What to match when buying

  • Match tank fixing (two bolts, plate or threaded nut), outlet position and outlet size.
  • Match type: manual or vacuum. Fitting a manual tap where a vacuum tap was fitted means you must turn it off every time.
  • Check the reserve tube length is similar to the old one, or reserve will start too early or too late.
  • Buy a new tank seal or O-ring with the tap.

Fitting notes

  • Home job: Check the fuel tap, lines and flow.
  • Drain the tank first, outside, away from flames. Do not overtighten into the tank.
  • After fitting, check flow in ON and RES into a container before connecting the hose.

Problems it can cause

  • Bogs down or chokes at full throttleRide with care
  • No fuel reaching the carburettorDo not ride
  • Fuel pours out of the carburettor overflow pipeDo not ride
  • Engine turns over but will not startDo not ride
  • Engine is flooded and smells of fuelDo not ride
  • Fuel is leaking or drippingStop now
  • Strong smell of fuel around the bikeDo not ride
  • Engine stops and only runs on reserve or after a restRide with care
  • Uses more fuel than normalOK to ride
  • Misfires or stutters when accelerating hard or climbingRide with care
  • Oil smells of petrol or the level risesDo not ride
  • Engine runs hot and lean, pops and pinksRide with care
  • Rust or flakes inside the fuel tankRide with care
  • Engine starts, then dies after a few secondsDo not ride
  • Sudden loss of power while ridingDo not ride
  • Water or dirt in the fuelDo not ride
