Wheels and tyres

Inner tube

Also called: tube

Find this part →See it in 3D

The inner tube is a rubber ring inside a tube-type (TT) tyre that holds the air. Its valve pokes through a hole in the rim. Most spoked wheels need a tube, because air leaks out through the spoke holes. A rubber rim tape protects the tube from the spoke nipples.

What it does

The tube holds air pressure; the tyre gives it shape and grip. A tube loses air fast when punctured, so a flat comes on suddenly. Tubes are pinched during fitting, chafed by sharp spoke ends or a missing rim tape, and damaged if ridden flat.

Signs it is worn or failing

  • Flat tyre: Flat tyre on a wheel with an inner tube.
  • Slow loss: Tyre slowly loses air.
  • Valve tilted or leaking: Air leaking from the tyre valve. A tilted valve means the tube has slipped on the rim; low pressure is the usual cause.

When to replace it

Replace a tube with a large cut, a split near the valve, many patches, or perished rubber. Fit a new tube with every new tyre. A small puncture can be patched, but a new tube is safer at speed.

What to match when buying

  • The tube size printed on it must match the tyre size, for example 2.75/3.00-17 or 90/90-17. Many tubes cover a range; check the tyre size falls in it.
  • Valve type and length: straight or bent. Match the old one so it reaches through the rim.
  • Never put a tube inside a tyre on a rim made for tubeless only unless the tyre maker allows it; the rim edge can chafe and overheat the tube.
  • Heavy duty tubes resist punctures better on rough roads. Avoid thin, cheap tubes with poor seams.

Fitting notes

  • Inflate a little to remove folds, then fit. Use levers carefully near the valve to avoid pinching.
  • Check the rim tape covers every nipple and no spoke end sticks out.
  • See Repair a puncture in an inner tube and Replace a tyre.
