Front sprocket

Also called: countershaft sprocket

Find this part →See it in 3DSee it in Under the Engine

Photo: Dan's Motorcycle Repair Course (dansmc.com), used with permission.

The front sprocket is the small toothed wheel on the end of the gearbox output shaft, on the left side of the engine behind a small cover. It is held by a bolt and plate or a nut and lock washer.

What it does

The gearbox turns the front sprocket, which pulls the chain. Because it is small, each tooth does more work than a tooth on the rear sprocket, so it often wears faster. The number of teeth sets the gearing: one tooth fewer gives stronger pulling but lower top speed and more fuel use.

Signs it is worn or failing

  • Teeth hooked, pointed or thin: Sprocket teeth hooked or broken.
  • Chain noise or jumping teeth: Chain noise, Chain jumps.
  • Oil behind the sprocket means the shaft seal, not the sprocket: Oil leaking behind the front sprocket.

When to replace it

Replace when teeth are hooked or bent, or about a quarter of the tooth is worn away. Always replace it with the chain and rear sprocket as a set.

What to match when buying

  • Chain size (for example 428), tooth count and spline pattern of the output shaft.
  • Offset (how far the teeth sit from the mounting face) must match, or the chain runs out of line.
  • Match make, model, year and engine code; the tooth count is usually stamped on the face.
  • Changing the tooth count changes the speedometer reading on bikes that measure speed at the gearbox.
  • Good sprockets have hardened, evenly cut teeth. Soft cheap sprockets wear in weeks.

Fitting notes

  • Use a new lock washer or plate if bent. Tighten the bolt to the value in your bike's manual.
  • Look at the output shaft seal while the sprocket is off.
  • See Inspect sprockets and Replace the chain and sprockets (workshop).

Problems it can cause

  • Chain is dry, rusty or has stiff linksRide with care
  • Chain jumps teeth or comes off the sprocketsStop now
  • Chain is noisy, clattering or slappingRide with care
  • Chain has snappedStop now
  • Chain needs adjusting very oftenRide with care
  • Chain is tight in some places and slack in othersRide with care
  • Whining noise in one or more gearsRide with care
  • Oil leaking behind the front sprocketRide with care
  • Struggles to climb hills or carry a passengerRide with care
  • Engine revs rise but the bike does not speed upRide with care
  • Sprocket teeth are hooked, sharp or brokenRide with care
  • Chain adjuster has run out of travelRide with care
