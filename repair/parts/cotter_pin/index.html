Cotter pins and slip pins

Also called: split pin, cotter, R-pin, slip pin

Find this part →

A cotter pin is a bent wire that passes through a hole in a shaft and through the slot of a castle nut. It stops the nut from turning off. A slip pin does a similar job on a clevis or a linkage.

What it does

It locks a nut in place when the torque alone is not enough, for example on a wheel axle or brake rod.

Signs it is worn or failing

  • Pin bent many times, cracked or rusted thin.
  • Pin missing, or worn through.
  • Nut loose: Nuts and bolts keep coming loose.

When to replace it

Fit a new pin each time the old one has been bent open and closed. A cheap new pin is better than a fatigued one.

What to match when buying

  • Diameter and length to suit the hole and the nut.
  • Stainless pins resist rust.
  • Keep a few spare in the tool kit.

Fitting notes

  • Round end forward, towards the front of the bike.
  • If the slots do not line up at the right torque, tighten further. Do not loosen the nut.
  • Bend the ends as the manual shows. A pin bent only slightly can be removed and reused.
  • See Fit and remove cotter pins.
