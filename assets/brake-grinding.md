# Brakes grind or scrape

A harsh grinding or scraping noise when you brake, often with a rough feeling in the lever, usually means the friction material is gone and the metal backing plate is cutting into the disc or drum. It can also be a stone or grit trapped between pad and disc, or a broken pad. Every stop now damages the disc and braking is getting weaker.

## Is it safe to ride?
No. Metal on metal stops poorly and can lock or break up. Push the bike to a place where you can fix it.

## Quick checks first
- Look into the caliper: can you see any friction material on both pads?
- Look at the disc: fresh deep, bright grooves?
- Does the noise happen with the brake off as well? Then something is rubbing all the time.
- Drum brake: does the wear pointer go past the end of its mark?

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. Is it a drum brake?**
- Yes: go to [Drum brake squeaks or grinds](/moto-zero/repair/problems/drum-brake-noise).
- No: go to step 2.

**2. Is either pad worn to the metal or below the limit (K-Light 125: 3 mm)?**
- Yes: [Replace disc brake pads](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/brake-pad-replace). Then go to step 4 to check the disc.
- No: go to step 3.

**3. Is a pad cracked, broken or loose on its plate, or is grit stuck between pad and disc?**
- Yes: remove the pads, clean the caliper, replace damaged pads. See [Clean a brake caliper and free the pistons](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/caliper-clean).
- No: go to step 5.

**4. After the metal touched the disc, is the disc deeply grooved or below its thickness limit?**
- Yes: replace the disc as well. New pads on a cut disc wear out fast and brake poorly. [Check a brake disc](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/brake-disc-check).
- No: light marks only; fit new pads and bed them in.

**5. Is the noise there with the brake released, and is one pad worn more than the other?**
- Yes: a sticking piston or slide keeps one pad on the disc. Go to [Brake drags and the wheel does not spin freely](/moto-zero/repair/problems/brake-drag).
- No: check that the caliper and disc bolts are tight and the disc is not touching the caliper body. A loose or bent part is a workshop check.

What happens if you keep riding: the steel backing plate eats the disc in a few days, a cheap pad job becomes a pad and disc job, and the piston can come out of its bore and dump the fluid.

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pads worn to metal | No friction material visible | [Replace disc brake pads](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/brake-pad-replace) |
| Disc cut by metal | Deep bright grooves; thin disc | [Check a brake disc](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/brake-disc-check) |
| Grit or broken pad | Stone or pad pieces seen | [Clean a brake caliper and free the pistons](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/caliper-clean) |
| Sticking caliper | Noise with brake off; uneven wear | [Clean a brake caliper and free the pistons](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/caliper-clean) |
| Worn drum shoes | Drum wear pointer past mark | [Service a drum brake](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/drum-brake-service) |
