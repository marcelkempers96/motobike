# Fuel tap

The fuel tap is a small valve under the tank of a carburettor bike, with a lever marked OFF, ON and RES (reserve). A vacuum tap has a small hose to the engine intake and opens only when the engine runs; some have a PRI (prime) position. Fuel-injected bikes have no tap; they use an in-tank [fuel pump](/moto-zero/repair/parts/fuel_pump).

## What it does
The tap lets fuel flow to the [carburettor](/moto-zero/repair/parts/carb) and stops it when the bike is parked. Two pickup tubes give a main supply and a lower reserve, so when the bike starts to stutter on ON, turning to RES gives a little more fuel. A strainer on the pickup and a sediment bowl catch dirt. Seals harden, strainers block, and vacuum diaphragms split.

## Signs it is worn or failing
- [No fuel reaching the carburettor](/moto-zero/repair/problems/carb-no-fuel).
- [Engine stops and only runs on reserve or after a rest](/moto-zero/repair/problems/fuel-starvation).
- [Fuel leaking](/moto-zero/repair/problems/fuel-leak) at the lever or tank joint.
- [Carb overflowing](/moto-zero/repair/problems/carb-overflowing) when parked on a vacuum tap that no longer shuts.

## When to replace it
When it leaks, will not shut, or still flows badly after the strainer is cleaned. Vacuum taps can sometimes be rebuilt with a diaphragm kit.

## What to match when buying
- Match tank fixing (two bolts, plate or threaded nut), outlet position and outlet size.
- Match type: manual or vacuum. Fitting a manual tap where a vacuum tap was fitted means you must turn it off every time.
- Check the reserve tube length is similar to the old one, or reserve will start too early or too late.
- Buy a new tank seal or O-ring with the tap.

## Fitting notes
- Home job: [Check the fuel tap, lines and flow](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fuel-tap-line-check).
- Drain the tank first, outside, away from flames. Do not overtighten into the tank.
- After fitting, check flow in ON and RES into a container before connecting the hose.
