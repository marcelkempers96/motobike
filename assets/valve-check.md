# Check and replace a tyre valve

**Home mechanic (basic tools)** · Time: 10 min to check; 30 to 60 min to replace (estimate) (estimate)


The valve lets air in and keeps it there. Leaks happen at the core (the small spring-loaded insert), at a cracked rubber stem, or where a tube valve joins the tube. A leaking valve is cheap to fix but feels just like a puncture.

## Safety
- Let the air out before removing a core: a core can shoot out under pressure.
- Replacing a tubeless valve needs one bead pushed off the rim. Support the bike firmly while the wheel is out.

## You need
- A valve core tool (some valve caps have one built in), spare cores, caps with seals.
- Soapy water.
- For tubeless snap-in valves: a new valve of the same type and a valve puller. **K-Light 125: snap-in tubeless valve Z2-01-1 (PDF p. 130).**
- For a tube: tube valves are part of the tube. A damaged tube valve means a new tube.

## Steps: check
1. Inflate the tyre to the correct pressure ([Check tyre pressure](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/tyre-pressure-check)).
2. Remove the cap. Put soapy water on the valve tip. Bubbles mean the core leaks.
3. Put soapy water round the base of the valve where it meets the rim. Bend a rubber valve gently to one side, then the other. Bubbles or cracks mean the stem is perished (weather cracks) or the seal in the rim is poor.
4. On a tube tyre, look at the angle of the valve. A valve leaning sideways means the tube has crept round the rim and may tear at the base.

## Steps: core leaks
1. Tighten the core gently with the core tool. Do not force it.
2. Test again. If it still bubbles, let the air out, unscrew the core and fit a new one.
3. Inflate, test, fit a cap with a seal.

## Steps: rubber tubeless valve cracked or leaking at the base
1. Remove the wheel ([front](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/wheel-remove-front), [rear](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/wheel-remove-rear)). Let all air out.
2. Push the bead next to the valve off the rim seat. You only need to reach the inside of the valve. Many riders let a tyre shop do this part.
3. Cut or pull out the old valve. Clean the hole in the rim.
4. Lubricate the new valve, push it into the hole from inside and pull it through with the puller until the groove seats on both sides of the rim.
5. Seat the bead, inflate, test with soapy water, set pressure.

## Steps: tube valve torn or leaning
1. Replace the tube: follow [Repair a puncture in an inner tube](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/tube-puncture-repair), fitting a new tube instead of a patch.

## Check your work
- No bubbles at the tip or base. Cap fitted.
- Pressure is the same after a night.

## If it did not fix the problem
- Still losing air with a good valve: [Tyre slowly loses air](/moto-zero/repair/problems/slow-puncture) or [Air leaking where the tyre meets the rim](/moto-zero/repair/problems/bead-leak).
