# Drive chain

The drive chain is the loop of steel links that runs from the small front sprocket on the engine to the large rear sprocket on the back wheel. It sits on the left side of most bikes, often under a chain guard or full chain case.

## What it does
Each link has two side plates, pins and rollers. The rollers sit in the sprocket teeth and pull the rear wheel round. The pins wear inside the bushes, so the chain gets longer: this is called stretch. A long chain rides higher on the teeth, wears the sprockets and can jump. Dirt, rain and missing oil speed up wear and cause stiff links.

## Signs it is worn or failing
- Noisy, clattering or slapping: [Chain noise](/moto-zero/repair/problems/chain-noise).
- Tight in one place and slack in another: [Chain tight spots](/moto-zero/repair/problems/chain-tight-spots).
- Needs adjusting very often: [Chain stretches fast](/moto-zero/repair/problems/chain-stretches-fast).
- Rusty or stiff links: [Chain dry or rusty](/moto-zero/repair/problems/chain-dry-rusty).
- Jumps teeth or comes off: [Chain jumps](/moto-zero/repair/problems/chain-jumps). Stop riding: a thrown chain can lock the rear wheel.
- Broken: [Chain has snapped](/moto-zero/repair/problems/chain-snapped).

## When to replace it
Replace when the adjusters run out of travel, the chain lifts off the back of the rear sprocket, links stay stiff after cleaning, or rollers or plates are cracked. Use the wear limit from the chain maker or your bike's manual. Replace the chain and both sprockets as a set; a new chain on old sprockets wears out fast.

## What to match when buying
- **Size code**, for example 428. The first digit is the pitch (pin to pin) in eighths of an inch: 4 means 1/2 inch, 5 means 5/8 inch. The last two digits give the inside width. 428, 520 and 530 chains do not swap.
- **Link count**, for example 134 links. Count the old chain or check the manual.
- Letters after the number show the type: H often means heavy duty; O-ring or X-ring chains have rubber seals that hold grease in.
- **Joining link**: clip type or rivet type. Rivet links need a press tool.
- Genuine or well-known brands print their name on every side plate. Be careful with very cheap chains, blurred printing and boxes with spelling mistakes.

## Fitting notes
- Clean and oil: [Clean and lubricate the chain](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-clean-lube). On O-ring chains use chain lube, not petrol or harsh solvent.
- Set slack: [Check and adjust chain slack](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-adjust). Measure wear: [Check the chain for wear](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-wear-check).
- Fitting a new chain and sprockets: workshop. See [Replace the chain and sprockets](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-sprocket-replace).
