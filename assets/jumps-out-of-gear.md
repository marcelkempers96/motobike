# Jumps out of gear under load

You are accelerating or climbing in a gear and it suddenly drops out, usually to neutral, and the engine revs. It often happens in the same gear every time. A gear is held in by its dogs (square teeth on the side of the gear) locking into slots on the next gear, and by a spring-loaded stopper arm that holds the drum in place. When the dogs round off, a fork wears or the stopper spring weakens, the load pushes the gear out. Before blaming the gearbox, rule out anything that stops the lever completing each change.

## Is it safe to ride?
Ride gently, straight to a workshop. A sudden loss of drive can throw you off balance, and each jump wears the dogs further. Avoid hard acceleration and steep hills with a passenger or load. Do not hold the lever with your foot as a fix.

## Quick checks first
- Is it always the same gear?
- Does your foot rest on the lever while riding?
- Is the lever loose, bent or touching anything?
- Was the bike dropped on the gear lever side?

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. Is your boot resting on the gear lever, or is the lever touching the footpeg or case?**
- Yes: move your foot clear and reset the lever height. [Check the gear lever and shift linkage](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearshift-check).
- No: go to step 2.

**2. Does the lever move on its shaft, or is it bent so it cannot complete the stroke?**
- Yes: [Refit or replace the gear lever](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gear-lever-replace).
- No: go to step 3.

**3. On a linkage, are the ball joints worn or the rod bent?**
- Yes: [Check the gear lever and shift linkage](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearshift-check).
- No: go to step 4.

**4. Do you change with a full, firm stroke and the clutch fully released? Does it still jump?**
- Yes, it still jumps: the fault is inside the engine. Go to step 5.
- No, it stopped: an incomplete change was leaving the dogs only partly engaged.

**5. Workshop: does the stopper arm spring or shift spindle return spring show weakness or breakage (checked behind the clutch cover)?**
- Yes: replacing the spring is the cheapest internal repair.
- No: the gearbox must be opened to check how far each gear engages, the dog and slot shapes, the splines and the fork wear. Go to [Assess internal gearbox faults](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearbox-assessment).

**What happens if you keep riding it:** each jump rounds the dogs further, so the gear jumps more easily, and a worn fork can overheat and break. A small repair (spring) becomes a large one (gears and forks).

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Foot resting on lever or lever obstructed | Visible; stops when foot moved | [Check the gear lever and shift linkage](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearshift-check) |
| Loose or bent lever | Lever moves on shaft or misses stroke | [Refit or replace the gear lever](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gear-lever-replace) |
| Weak or broken stopper arm spring | Workshop check behind clutch cover | [Assess internal gearbox faults](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearbox-assessment) |
| Rounded dogs, flared slots | Gearbox opened | [Assess internal gearbox faults](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearbox-assessment) |
| Worn or bent fork, worn drum groove | Measured against limits | [Assess internal gearbox faults](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearbox-assessment) |
