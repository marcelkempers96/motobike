# Whining noise in one or more gears

A whine or howl that you hear in certain gears, which rises and falls with road speed. Gear teeth and gearbox bearings whine when they wear, pit or lack oil. But a dry or tight chain, a worn wheel bearing and engine noises can sound very similar, so the first job is to find where the noise really comes from. A faint whine has always been there on some engines; a new or growing one is the warning.

## Is it safe to ride?
Ride with care. A whine alone rarely stops the bike, but if it turns into grinding, knocking or the gears feel rough, stop: a breaking gear or bearing can lock the gearbox and the rear wheel.

## Quick checks first
- Check the oil level.
- Look at the chain: dry, rusty or too tight?
- Note which gears whine and at what speed.

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. On a quiet road, ride at a steady speed where you hear it. Pull the clutch and let the engine idle while you roll. Does the whine stop?**
- Yes: it comes from the engine or gearbox, which only carry load when the clutch is engaged. Go to step 2.
- No, it continues at the same road speed with the clutch pulled: it comes from the chain, wheels or tyres. Go to step 5.

**2. Does the whine change when you change gear at the same road speed?**
- Yes: likely the gearbox. Go to step 3.
- No, it follows engine revs even in neutral at standstill: it is engine noise. Go to [Whining noise from the engine](/moto-zero/repair/problems/whine-from-engine).

**3. Is the oil level correct and the oil clean?**
- No: [Check the engine oil level](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/oil-level-check) and top up or change it. The gearbox shares the engine oil on most commuter bikes.
- Yes: go to step 4.

**4. Drain or sample the oil. Is there glitter, flakes or chunks of metal?**
- Yes: stop riding. See [Metal particles or flakes in the oil](/moto-zero/repair/problems/metal-in-oil) and [Inspect drained oil for clues](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/oil-inspect).
- No: a gear or bearing is likely wearing. Go to [Assess internal gearbox faults](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearbox-assessment). Note which gears whine; it helps the workshop.

**5. Is the chain dry, rusty or too tight?**
- Yes: [Clean and lubricate the chain](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-clean-lube), [Check and adjust chain slack](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-adjust).
- No: go to step 6.

**6. With the bike on the stand, does a wheel feel rough or rumbly when you spin it by hand, or does it have play?**
- Yes: [Check wheel bearings](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/wheel-bearing-check); see [Rumbling or grinding from a wheel](/moto-zero/repair/problems/wheel-bearing-noise).
- No: use a [Road test to diagnose a fault](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/road-test) with an experienced mechanic.

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dry or tight chain | Noise stays with clutch pulled | [Clean and lubricate the chain](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-clean-lube) |
| Low or old oil | Level low, oil dark | [Check the engine oil level](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/oil-level-check) |
| Wheel bearing | Rough when spun by hand | [Check wheel bearings](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/wheel-bearing-check) |
| Worn gear teeth or gearbox bearing | Whine in certain gears, clutch-dependent | [Assess internal gearbox faults](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearbox-assessment) |
