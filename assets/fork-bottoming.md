# Front forks bottom out

On a bump or under hard braking the forks compress all the way and stop with a hard clunk. The front may also dive a long way when you brake, or keep bouncing after a bump. The fork has too little oil (from a leak), old thin oil, tired springs, or it is carrying more weight than it was set up for. The spring holds the bike up; the oil slows the movement. Low or old oil means little damping, and tired springs mean the fork sits lower and has less travel left.

## Is it safe to ride?
Ride with care at lower speed. Hard bottoming under braking can upset the bike. If one leg leaks heavily or oil is on the brake, **do not ride**.

## Quick checks first
- Look for oil on the fork tubes and legs.
- Hold the front brake and push the bars down hard: does it go down too easily, or knock at the bottom?
- Push down and let go: does it bounce more than once?
- Is the bike carrying heavy loads on the front (bags on the bars, a front rack)?

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. Is either fork leg leaking?**
- Yes: the leg has lost oil. Go to [Oil leaking from a fork leg](/moto-zero/repair/problems/fork-leak).
- No: go to step 2.

**2. Does the front bounce more than once after you push down and let go?**
- Yes: damping is weak: low, old or wrong-grade oil. [Replace fork oil and seals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fork-oil-seals).
- No: go to step 3.

**3. Does the front sit lower than before, or does the fork use almost all its travel just with the rider on board?**
- Yes: the springs are tired. Check with [Measure suspension sag](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/sag-measure). The workshop measures spring free length (K-Light 125: 300 mm new, replace below 295 mm).
- No: go to step 4.

**4. Do both legs feel the same?**
- No: one leg may have less oil, a broken spring or a damaged damper. Workshop: [Replace fork oil and seals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fork-oil-seals).
- Yes: go to step 5.

**5. Is the load heavier than the bike was built for, or is the front tyre soft?**
- Yes: reduce the load and set the tyre pressure with [Check tyre pressure](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/tyre-pressure-check). A soft rear can also tip weight forward; see [Rear sits too low](/moto-zero/repair/problems/rear-sagging).
- No: have a workshop check the oil amount and grade is exactly as the manual says. Wrong amount or grade is common after a cheap service.

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Oil lost through leaking seals | Oil on tubes | [Replace fork oil and seals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fork-oil-seals) |
| Old or wrong oil | Bounces; never changed | [Replace fork oil and seals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fork-oil-seals) |
| Tired springs | Sits low; large sag | [Measure suspension sag](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/sag-measure), springs replaced in pairs |
| Overload or soft tyre | Load; gauge reading | Reduce load, [Check tyre pressure](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/tyre-pressure-check) |
| Damaged damper in one leg | Legs feel different | Workshop |
