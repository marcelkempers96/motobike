Under the Engine

Everything on this stand is one engine: a 125 cc air-cooled single-cylinder four-stroke of the unit-construction type that powers most commuter and boda boda motorcycles, where crankshaft, generator, both starters, clutch and the five-speed constant-mesh gearbox share one crankcase. The cases and covers are removed so every gear, chain, fork and roller moves in sync with the crank. Start it, pull the clutch, shift through the box and click any part for its job and tooth count.

Reference engine

125 cc OHC single, five speeds

Bore 56.5 mm, stroke 49.5 mm, chain-driven overhead cam with two valves, magneto flywheel with AC CDI ignition, wet multi-plate clutch and a return-shift five-speed box (1-N-2-3-4-5). Tooth counts are chosen to give ratios typical of this class of engine; the tables below list every one.

Parts by system

Gearbox ratios

Every gear pair has the same 49-tooth sum at module 2.0, which is what lets all five pairs sit on two shafts 49 mm apart and stay in constant mesh. Road speed assumes a 2.75-18 rear tyre with a rolling circumference of 1.95 m.

How the drum selects each gear

Only one dog clutch is ever engaged. Every other gear spins freely on its shaft while staying in mesh.
