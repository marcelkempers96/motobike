## How this research was done

- Discovery used public web searches and 24 YouTube query themes spanning maintenance, engineering, repairs, restorations, custom builds, electrical diagnosis, fuel systems, scooter transmissions, Hindi engine repair and Indonesian restoration.
- The discovery pass produced 344 distinct candidate video IDs. Candidates near or above the threshold were checked individually; the final 100 all passed the 500,000-view requirement on their watch pages.
- Exact titles, channel names/IDs, view counts, durations, publication dates, exposed likes, uploader keywords, thumbnail URLs and available caption-track language codes were extracted from public watch-page metadata. Subscriber counts, revenue and private analytics were not used.
- **Descriptions below are original research summaries based on the title, creator description and any chapters available in that description. These are not full-video reviews or transcript-verified summaries.** Where descriptions are mostly promotional or sparse, the summary stays at topic level. Learning notes are editorial suggestions for Moto Zero, not claims that every detail was demonstrated or technically validated.
- The set favours motorcycle-specific instruction and engineering. One transparent-carburettor science demonstration is included as a directly transferable mechanism lesson. General car videos, generic reviews, unrelated riding clips, bicycle-only maintenance and obvious duplicate re-edits were excluded from the final selection. Separate related uploads are retained only as distinct records; footage overlap has not been exhaustively checked.
- Language codes below identify caption tracks exposed during retrieval, not a full language audit. No caption track exposed does not prove that a video has no captions. Exact source titles are retained, including non-English titles.
- No videos were downloaded or reuploaded. Every entry links to its source and channel. Before embedding, check availability and creator/platform settings. A public thumbnail URL or video link is not a licence to republish imagery or footage.

## Main findings for Moto Zero

**1. Give beginners a visible system map.** Whole-motorcycle animations, clutch animations and transmission explanations have large audiences in this sample. Pair these with your parts directory: component name → function → symptoms → checks → suitable repair.

**2. Keep routine maintenance easy to find.** Chain care, carburettor cleaning, oil changes, clutch adjustment and electrical checks provide direct matches to existing repair pages. These are stronger immediate library candidates than a spectacular build with little explanation.

**3. Separate inspiration from instruction.** Restoration and factory-assembly videos can be highly engaging, but time-lapse and edited process films omit measurements, inspection decisions and failed attempts. Present them as case studies unless qualified review confirms they are suitable tutorials.

**4. Include commuter motorcycles.** Small Honda/Hero-family engines, underbones and 70 cc assembly videos broaden the library beyond large Western-market motorcycles. Model, year and market identification remain necessary; a similar-looking engine does not establish identical parts or procedures.

**5. Engineering disagreements are useful teaching material.** The twin-engine balance correction included here shows why credible explanations should cite assumptions and evidence. Do not equate a million views with correctness.

**6. Modifications need a purpose and a boundary.** Organise upgrades by comfort, maintenance, handling, power or appearance. Historical price claims, legality claims and performance claims need separate verification. Titles are reproduced as titles, not endorsed promises.

**7. Study presentation, not imagined analytics.** The sample includes comparison tests, numbered lists, cutaways, ASMR, time-lapse and will-it-run narratives. These are visible editorial formats. Retention, click-through rate, audience location and the reasons for success are private or unmeasured here.

## How to connect this research to the website

| Video topic | Suggested Moto Zero destination | Editorial requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Overall construction and component function | `/overview/` and `/repair/parts/` | An HTML explanation and component links should accompany the video |

| Carburettor cleaning / pilot circuit | `/repair/repairs/carburettor-clean/` and relevant starting/idle symptom pages | State carburettor design and applicability |

| Oil and filter service | `/repair/repairs/engine-oil-service/` | Distinguish filters, strainers, oil types and model quantities |
| Clutch / gearbox / scooter CVT | Component explanations and drivetrain lessons | Separate these transmission types rather than presenting one universal system |
| Restoration / custom builds | Model-specific case-study pages | Link component lessons; avoid implying the footage is a complete service manual |
| Engineering comparisons | Engine and chassis lessons | Add primary technical references and identify contested claims |

Suggested database fields for implementation: `video_id`, `title`, `channel_id`, `channel_name`, `source_url`, `published_at`, `duration_seconds`, `views_observed`, `likes_observed`, `checked_at`, `topic`, `format`, `summary`, `editorial_learning`, `caption_languages_observed`, `part_ids`, `model_variant_ids`, `technical_review_status`, `embed_status`, `rights_notes`.

Use a join table for video-to-part and video-to-model links: one video can cover several parts, and a part can have several suitable videos. Keep topical relevance separate from compatibility. For example, a CG125 rebuild can illustrate single-cylinder construction without becoming an authoritative procedure for every CG-derived engine.

## Review checklist before publication

1. Watch the complete selected video and verify that it matches the intended lesson.
2. Record relevant timestamps manually; do not manufacture chapters from a title.
3. Identify the demonstrated model, variant and procedure limitations.
4. Have a qualified reviewer check technical and safety-critical statements against suitable references.
5. Confirm embedding availability and attribution. Do not copy a video, transcript or thumbnail merely because it is publicly accessible.
6. Write a short original explanation on the page, with links to related concepts and procedures.
7. Recheck availability, metadata and counts periodically; keep the original observation date in the database.

## Limits and exclusions

This research verifies the selected videos' metadata and their relevance at a title/description level. It does not certify technical accuracy, establish who owns all depicted footage, audit every spoken claim, or prove restoration authenticity. Creator marketing claims such as “best”, “secret”, “triple lifespan” or “worth the ticket” are not adopted as findings.

Popular videos below 500,000 observed views were excluded, even where their topic was useful. Counts from search snippets were not substituted when individual watch-page verification was available. A large re-edited restoration upload was excluded in favour of the source creator's project, and bicycle-only and general vehicle videos were removed from the selection. Distinct related videos may still cover the same project or topic; the list is a research library rather than 100 independent technical experiments.

The language mix is mainly English-facing material with selected Hindi/Hinglish and globally accessible process videos. It is not an exhaustive multilingual survey. No claims are made about audience demographics, revenue, retention, watch time or expected traffic to Moto Zero.
