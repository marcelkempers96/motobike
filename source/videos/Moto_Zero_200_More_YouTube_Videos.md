## What is verified, and what is interpretation?

- **Verified:** source URL/video ID, observed views, title, channel identity, duration and publication metadata. Likes, caption-language codes and uploader keywords are included where exposed. Counts were checked on 6 November 2026; some same-day records from the earlier research pass were reused.
- **Research summaries:** original concise descriptions based on the title and available creator metadata. They are topic-level descriptions, not transcript-verified accounts or full-video reviews. Sparse metadata is not filled with invented procedure details.
- **Learning notes:** editorial suggestions for how Moto Zero could use each topic. These are not statements that the video proves a claim or that its instructions have been technically approved.
- **Not verified:** every spoken technical claim, restoration authenticity, original footage ownership, all workshop safety practices, actual performance gains, legal status, private analytics or expected website traffic.

Deduplication is by **YouTube video ID**, not by title. Different tutorials can have the same title. Known series relationships are noted; this is **200 additional uploads, not 200 unrelated projects or independently validated techniques**. ## Main learning and editorial opportunities

1. **Add a second level beneath basic maintenance.** The new fork, bearing, brake-caliper and valve-clearance videos extend the first collection's oil, chain and basic carburettor coverage. Keep these advanced tasks distinct from beginner checks.
2. **Create fault-led journeys.** Will-it-run projects and seized-engine cases can illustrate diagnostic reasoning. Extract the actual test sequence only after full viewing; an exciting title is not a diagnosis.
3. **Connect animation with real hardware.** ABS, quickshifter, clutch, rotary-engine and desmodromic-valve explainers can complement component photographs and workshop examples.
4. **Use manufacturing to teach component relationships.** Factory videos show assemblies in context, but production methods and repair methods are not interchangeable.
5. **Build regional and model diversity.** Small Honda/Hero-family motorcycles, scooters, underbones, classic British and European bikes, sportbikes and custom American machines all appear. Check exact variants before connecting to model-specific parts.
6. **Present electric work as advanced case studies.** Conversion and battery-repair narratives can explain integration constraints. Do not convert them into general DIY traction-battery instructions without qualified technical review.
7. **Keep claims attached to their evidence.** Product comparisons, power claims, mileage promises, prices and legal claims remain the creators' claims until independently checked. Views measure attention, not accuracy.
8. **Avoid empty video pages.** Add original context, a diagram, model applicability and links to useful repair/parts pages rather than publishing an embed alone for every upload.

## Limitations

This is a broad, curated research collection with a verified minimum-view threshold. It is not an exhaustive multilingual ranking, a technical certification or a prediction of traffic. Search discovery and the available metadata favour some channels and formats. The largest restoration audiences do not establish the best instruction quality.

Exact model names in titles can be imprecise: for example, a replica minibike is not the full-size sportbike whose styling it imitates. Some uploads are episodes of the same project, and manufacturing footage may be distributed by an uploader other than the manufacturer. These issues need editorial review before model tagging or embedding.

No private analytics, audience demographics, earnings, retention, click-through rates or verified conversion outcomes were available. Caption-track presence does not establish transcript accuracy or complete language coverage. View counts and availability can change after the observation date.
