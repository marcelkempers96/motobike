## Keep or skip
Keep: real parts, tools in use, measurements, adjustments, wear and damage, clear diagrams drawn
for the course.
Skip (and count in the notes): scans of manufacturer service manual pages or wiring diagrams
copied from a brand's manual, magazine or advert scans, old-bike history and museum photos, whole
bikes with no repair point, people as the subject, jokes, cartoons, screenshots, near-duplicates.
In a numbered series (`_2.jpg`, `_3.jpg` ...) keep only the best 1 or 2 unless each shows a
different step.

## Where to place a photo (in this order)
1. An existing repair page whose job the photo shows (a step, tool or measurement). Up to 8 photos
   per repair page in total, counting those already in photo-map.json and pass 1 frontmatter.
2. An existing part page showing that part clearly. Up to 6 per part page in total.
3. An existing problem page showing the fault or damage (worn, broken, burnt). Up to 4 per page.
4. A system page (e.g. ignition, brakes) for a good general photo. Up to 6 per system.
5. A course chapter where it explains a principle in the text. Up to 3 per chapter from your group.
6. A NEW page (below) when several good photos show a job or fault that has no page.
A photo can go in more than one place only if it fits both well (at most 2 places).

## New pages
Write 5 to 15 new pages (repairs, and problems where a fault has no page), only where you have
good photos and the course text explains the job. These must be proper repair pages, the same as
pass 1: read `source/repair/WRITER_BRIEF.md` (formats and writing rules; binding, except the
registry path is `source/repair/briefs/existing-ids.md` and its logs/PDF paths do not apply), and
the examples `source/repair/md/repairs/chain-adjust.md` and
`source/repair/new/05_Electrical_Ignition/repairs/battery-dry-activate.md` (frontmatter with
`photos:`). Every new page needs: You need, Safety where relevant, numbered steps, checks or a
"how to tell" table, when to go to a workshop. Language: plain, short, calm, impersonal. No first
person, no anecdotes, no humour, no opinions, no "Dan". Paraphrase; do not copy sentences.
Numbers: only general facts from the source that are not model-specific; otherwise "use the value
in your bike's manual". Never invent numbers. No em or en dashes.
Sources: `{doc: "Dan's Motorcycle Repair Course", url: "<page_url>"}`.
Do not duplicate an existing or pass 1 page: check the registry titles first.
