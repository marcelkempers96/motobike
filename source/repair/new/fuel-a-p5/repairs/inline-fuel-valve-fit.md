# Fit an inline shut-off valve in place of a failed fuel tap

Fuel taps are costly for what they are. If a tap flows but will not shut off, a plain plastic inline valve fitted in the hose to the carburettor is a cheap fix. It is sold for garden machines. It gives on and off only, with no reserve, so it suits a bike that is kept topped up. It is also a stopgap for a tap that cannot be repaired.

## Safety
- Fuel burns. Work outdoors, no flames, and fuel-safe containers.
- Use only a valve and hose that are made for petrol. Garden water fittings are not suitable.
- Check for leaks before every ride, and check the valve for cracks.

## You need
- A plastic inline fuel valve to match the hose bore.
- Fuel hose, and new clamps (not worn or rusted ones).
- A container and rags.

## Steps
1. Turn off the tap, or if it will not close, lower the fuel level by draining the tank into a container. See [Drain old or contaminated fuel](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/drain-fuel).
2. Pull the fuel hose off the carburettor and catch the fuel.
3. Cut the hose near the carburettor end. Clamp the valve into the line with the arrow, if present, pointing to the carburettor.
4. Fit a fuel filter in the line if the tap is still in service. It keeps dirt from the carburettor.
5. Route the hose with no kinks, away from the exhaust and moving parts.
6. Turn the valve on and look at every joint for several minutes.
7. Turn the valve off whenever the bike is parked.

## Check your work
- No fuel at the joints. The engine runs with the valve fully open.
- Closing the valve stops the fuel; the engine runs out and stops.

## If it did not fix the problem
- Flooding continues with the valve off: [Check the carburettor float and needle valve](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/carb-float-check).
- No fuel with the valve open: [Check the fuel tap, lines and flow](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fuel-tap-line-check).
