# Measure bolt stretch and learn what a failed bolt looks like

A bolt acts like a spring. When it is tightened it stretches a little, and this stretch holds the parts together. A bolt can be checked by turning force (torque) or by its length (stretch). Some connecting rod bolts are set by stretch: the manual says if so, and gives the length.

## Practice on a junk bolt
1. Measure the length of an old bolt with calipers or a micrometer: [Choose and use calipers and micrometers](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/measuring-tools-use). Write it down.
2. Put washers and a nut on it and clamp the head in a vice.
3. Tighten the nut to the torque in your manual for that size and grade: [Use a torque wrench correctly](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/torque-wrench-use).
4. Measure the bolt again. It will be a little longer.
5. Loosen it and measure it a third time. A good bolt returns to its first length. A bolt that stays longer has failed.
6. Tighten a second bolt until it gives way, so you know how it feels when a bolt yields: the wrench turns on without the force rising.

## In the engine
- If a bolt gives way (yields) as you tighten it, replace it. Do not reuse it.
- Rod bolts, cylinder head bolts and studs are under high stress. Replace any that are stretched or damaged: [Bolts, nuts, studs and washers](/moto-zero/repair/parts/fasteners).
- Check rod bolt stretch only where the manual calls for it.

## Check your work
- You know how long a good bolt is, and what a bolt that has taken a set looks like.
