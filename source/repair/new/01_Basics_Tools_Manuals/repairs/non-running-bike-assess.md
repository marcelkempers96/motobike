# Assess a bike that does not run before you buy or restore it

A bike that has stood for months or years needs restoration, not just repair. Rust forms, rubber dries and fuel turns to varnish. A short set of checks shows whether the bike is worth the cost. A non-running bike is worth much less than a running one, so settle the price from what you find.

## Safety
- Keep a fire extinguisher near. Sparks and backfires can ignite old fuel.
- Do not crank a stuck engine with the starter.
- Never connect a charged battery to a dead one in the bike. Remove the dead battery first. A flat battery can overheat and burst.
- Test brakes and tyres before riding. Old tyres can crack inside.

## You need
- A torch, notebook, plug spanner and compression gauge.
- A charged battery of the right voltage, or a charger.
- A clean jar to catch fuel and oil samples.

## Steps
1. **Look it over.** Check for bent parts, scrapes and fresh metal where it has fallen. Check frame, forks, bars and wheels for crash damage: [Inspect the frame after a crash](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/frame-crash-inspect). Note rust and missing parts.
2. **Ask the history.** Treat a seller's story with care and check facts yourself. Low mileage on a very old bike may simply mean it stood for years.
3. **Oil.** Check level and colour: [Inspect drained oil for clues](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/oil-inspect). Milky oil means water. A fuel smell means a leaking fuel valve. Burnt smell can mean clutch trouble.
4. **Turn it over.** Remove the battery. Use a charged battery of the correct voltage, or kick it over. If it will not turn: [Find out why the engine will not turn over](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/engine-stuck-check). A stuck engine means a top end rebuild.
5. **Compression.** Remove the plug, ground the plug lead, open the throttle fully and crank. Use a gauge: [Test engine compression](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/compression-test). A rough check without a gauge is to put a thumb over the plug hole while cranking. Firm pressure that pushes the thumb off suggests there is some compression, but it does not replace a gauge.
6. **Cylinder pressure leak.** If compression is low on a four-stroke, check valve clearance first: [Check and adjust valve clearance](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/valve-clearance). Then use air through the plug hole at top dead centre to find where it escapes: air heard at the intake means the intake valve, at the exhaust the exhaust valve, at the breather the rings. A workshop can do this: [Do a leak-down test](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/leak-down-test).
7. **Spark.** Check for a spark: [Check for a spark safely](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/spark-test). If there is none on an electronic ignition bike, repairs can be costly: [Diagnose a suspected CDI or ECU fault](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/cdi-ecu-diagnosis).
8. **Fuel and carburettor.** Expect old fuel. Drain it and check for water: [Test fuel for water](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fuel-water-test), [Bring a bike back into use after storage](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/recommission-after-storage).
9. **Price it.** Add up the likely cost of the repairs: piston and rings, carburettor work, tyres, chain, battery. Compare with what a running bike of the same type sells for. Walk away if parts and labour make it a poor deal.
10. **After it runs,** do the basic service only, then ride a few hundred kilometres. Several small faults clear by themselves. Fix what remains. Replace dangerous items at once: cracked tyres and a worn or stiff chain.

## Check your work
- You have a written list of faults and their cost.
- You know if the engine has compression and spark, and whether the gearbox, clutch and brakes work.

## If it did not fix the problem
- Engine turns over but still will not start: [Engine turns over but will not start](/moto-zero/repair/problems/cranks-no-start).
