# Buy, borrow or make a special tool

Some jobs need a tool made for one task. A flywheel puller, a clutch nut tool and a bearing driver are common examples. Before a job, decide whether the tool is needed, how to get it, and whether a substitute is safe.

## Safety
- A wrong substitute can crack a flywheel, bend a shaft or wreck a bearing. A broken part costs more than the tool.
- Do not use a general puller on a part the manual says needs its own puller.

## You need
- Your bike's workshop manual (it names the special tools).
- A list of the jobs you plan to do.

## Steps
1. Read the job steps first. Note every tool the manual names, and which steps cannot be done without it.
2. Sort each tool into one of three groups:
   - Fits one procedure on one engine or frame only (for example a clutch nut socket with a special thread).
   - Fits one procedure on several models from one maker (for example most flywheel pullers).
   - Fits one procedure on many makes (for example a piston pin puller).
3. Group 3 is worth buying: it will work on many bikes. Group 2 can be shared. Group 1 is best borrowed or hired, or made.
4. Ask other mechanics, a bike club or a nearby workshop to lend or hire the tool.
5. Buy a general tool if you will use it often. Cheaper copies often work if they fit properly.
6. If the job cannot wait and the tool cannot be had, consider making one: [Make a simple special tool](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/make-simple-special-tool).
7. Do not force a substitute. If the part does not move under normal force with the right tool, stop and re-read the steps: heat, a missed bolt or a locking pin may be the cause.
8. Before you start, check that the tool fits the part and covers all the threads or flats it needs.

## Check your work
- The tool fits the part with no gaps, and the force goes through the part it is meant to hold.
- The part came off or went on without damage.

## If it did not fix the problem
- No tool and no way to make one: take the part to a workshop that has it.
