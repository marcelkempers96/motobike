# Wheel hub or brake drum is very hot after riding

After a ride, one wheel hub or brake drum is much hotter than the other. A little warmth is normal where brakes were used. A hub too hot to keep your hand on, with little braking, means friction where there should be none. The two usual causes are a brake that drags and a bearing that has lost its grease.

## Is it safe to ride?
No. A seized brake can lock the wheel, and a failed bearing can let the wheel tilt or stop. Let the wheel cool and fix the cause first.

## Quick checks first
- Wait until the wheel is cool before touching it.
- Spin the wheel by hand with the bike on a stand. It should turn freely and run on for a while.
- Pull the brake lever or press the pedal and release. The wheel should be free again.
- Look for blue or brown heat marks on the axle or disc.

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. Does the wheel spin freely with the bike on the stand and the brake released?**
- No, it drags or stops quickly: go to step 2.
- Yes: go to step 3.

**2. Is the wheel a drum brake or a disc brake?**
- Drum: the cable or rod may be too tight, the pedal or lever may not return, or the cam may be stiff. Follow [Adjust a drum brake](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/drum-brake-adjust) and, for a twin leading shoe brake, [Adjust a twin leading shoe drum brake](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/drum-brake-twin-leading-adjust).
- Disc: a caliper that sticks or a master cylinder that does not release holds the pads on the disc. Go to [Check for brake drag](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/brake-drag-check).
- If drag stays after this, see [Brake drags and the wheel does not spin freely](/moto-zero/repair/problems/brake-drag).

**3. Does the wheel rock on its axle or feel rough when you turn it?**
- Yes: a bearing is worn or dry. Go to [Check wheel bearings](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/wheel-bearing-check).
- No: go to step 4.

**4. Was the wheel recently refitted, and was the drum brake centred when the axle was tightened?**
- It may have been tightened with the shoes off-centre. Loosen the axle, apply the brake and tighten it again. See [Centre the brake shoes when refitting a drum wheel](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/drum-brake-centre-shoes).
- No recent work: go to step 5.

**5. Is the hub still too hot after the brake is clear and the bearings feel smooth?**
- Replace the bearings anyway if there is any doubt. A bearing that has overheated is ruined even if it feels smooth. See [Replace wheel bearings](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/wheel-bearing-replace).
- Heat discolouration on the axle also means bearing and axle must be replaced.

If you keep riding on a hot, dry bearing, it will fail. If a long trip is planned and you cannot remember the last bearing change, replace them first.

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Drum brake adjusted too tight | Wheel drags, drum hot with little braking | [Adjust a drum brake](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/drum-brake-adjust) |
| Brake shoes off-centre in the drum | Started after the wheel was refitted | [Centre the brake shoes when refitting a drum wheel](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/drum-brake-centre-shoes) |
| Sticking caliper or pads | Disc hot, pads drag | [Check for brake drag](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/brake-drag-check) |
| Dry or worn bearing | Wheel rough or loose, brake is clear | [Replace wheel bearings](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/wheel-bearing-replace) |
| Overheated bearing | Axle discoloured, hub very hot | Replace bearings and axle |
