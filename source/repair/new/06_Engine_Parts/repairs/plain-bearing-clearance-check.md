# Check plain bearing clearance with Plastigauge

Plain bearings (also called shell or bush bearings) have no balls or rollers. The shaft floats on a thin film of oil held under pressure between it and a shell or the head metal. They last very long while oil and oil pressure are good. Wear shows as knocking, a dropping oil pressure light, or metal in the oil. Plastigauge is a thin plastic thread that is squashed by the bearing cap and then compared with a chart, so clearance can be read without special gauges.

## Safety
- This job is done with the engine apart. Follow the manual for how to open the engine.
- The thread squashed by the cap is only a measurement aid. Clean it off before final assembly.
- Never turn the shaft while the thread is under the cap. That ruins the reading.

## You need
- Plastigauge in the range that covers the clearance in the manual. It comes in several sizes, with the scale printed on the envelope in millimetres and inches.
- The clearance limit, cap tightening order and torque from the manual.
- A clean bench. Oil and dirt will falsify the result.

## Steps
1. Look first. Plain bearing shells that have worn through show a different coloured layer, or have hammered or spun in their housing. If the shells are plainly damaged, replace them. No measurement is needed.
2. Wipe the shaft journal and the bearing shell dry and clean. Do not oil them.
3. Cut a length of Plastigauge slightly shorter than the bearing width. Lay it along the journal, away from any oil hole.
4. Fit the cap or the head cover and tighten the bolts to the manual torque, in the stated order. Do not turn the shaft.
5. Remove the cap again, gently, without moving the shaft.
6. Compare the width of the flattened thread with the scale on the envelope. A wider flat thread means less clearance. Read the nearest mark.
7. Compare the reading with the manual.
   - Within limit: the shell can be reused. Clean off all the plastic with a fingernail or a soft cloth.
   - Too large: fit a new shell. On some engines a slightly thicker shell is sold. Fit it and measure again.
   - Still too large, or the journal is scored or worn: the crankshaft needs regrinding to an undersize and undersize shells fitted, if the maker sells them. Many engines do not. A crankshaft may then have to be replaced.
8. On a bearing with no cap (a camshaft that runs straight in the head), Plastigauge cannot be used. Check by feel and sight: the cam should rock only a very small amount in its bore, and rocker shafts almost not at all. A workshop can measure with an expanding ball gauge and micrometer.
9. Never run low on oil. Wear of this type begins at start-up before oil pressure rises, and lasts as long as the oil supply is good.

## Check your work
- Each reading is written down, with the bearing it came from.
- Shells are fitted in their original places and the right way round.
- Oil passages are clean, and the engine is filled with clean oil before the first start.
