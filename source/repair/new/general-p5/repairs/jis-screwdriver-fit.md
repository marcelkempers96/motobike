# Choose the right Phillips bit for Japanese bike screws (JIS)

Most bike crankcase and cover screws are Phillips head, but of the JIS type. Many Phillips bits do not fit them well. A loose bit slips out (cams out) and rounds the head and the bit. That turns a two minute job into a drilling job.

## You need
- A bit or screwdriver made for JIS screws. A plain "Phillips" bit may or may not fit.
- An impact driver for tight screws, held square to the screw.

## Steps
1. Find a screw that is in good shape and test the bit in it. It must sit tight and snug with no wobble.
2. If the bit rocks or is loose, it is the wrong type. Find another bit. This is much quicker than drilling out a ruined screw head.
3. JIS screws are sometimes marked with a small punch mark on the head, but many are not. Do not rely on the mark.
4. ISO Phillips screws have a larger head than JIS. They are not the same, and JIS is the type used on most motorcycles.
5. In your parts shop, ask for a JIS screwdriver or bit and a size. The packaging may not say JIS. A size No. 3 bit often suits larger screws and a No. 2 suits the small ones.
6. Push hard into the screw as you turn. For a tight screw, strike the impact driver with a hammer while you turn.
7. Do not press the bit on a damaged head. Move to [Screw or bolt will not come out](/moto-zero/repair/problems/fastener-stuck-rounded).

## Check your work
- The screw turns out without damage to its head.
- Keep the bit that fits as your JIS bit and mark it.

## If it did not fix the problem
- Rounded head: add a little valve grinding paste into the recess to help the bit bite, or use [Free a stuck fastener](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/stuck-fastener-removal).
- Impact driver tips: [Use an impact driver](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/impact-driver-use).
