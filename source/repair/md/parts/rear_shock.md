# Rear shock absorbers

Rear shock absorbers connect the swingarm (or scooter engine) to the frame. Most commuter bikes have two, one each side; some have a single shock (monoshock) under the seat. Each one is a coil spring around a sealed oil damper.

## What it does
The spring holds the bike up and soaks up bumps. The damper slows the spring down so the bike does not keep bouncing. Many shocks have a preload adjuster (a stepped collar) to set the spring for a passenger or load. Over time the damper oil leaks or wears out and the spring sags.

## Signs it is worn or failing
- Rear keeps bouncing after a bump: [Rear bounces](/moto-zero/repair/problems/rear-bouncy).
- Rear sits low: [Rear sits too low](/moto-zero/repair/problems/rear-sagging).
- Harsh ride: [Rear suspension feels harsh](/moto-zero/repair/problems/rear-harsh).
- Oil on the shock body: [Oil leaking from a rear shock](/moto-zero/repair/problems/shock-leak).
- Clunk: [Clunk from the rear suspension](/moto-zero/repair/problems/rear-clunk). Check the mounting bushes.

## When to replace it
Most commuter shocks are sealed and cannot be rebuilt. Replace a shock that leaks, has a bent rod, a broken spring or no damping. Replace twin shocks as a pair.

## What to match when buying
- Make, model and year. Eye-to-eye length (centre of top mount to centre of bottom mount), mount type (eye or stud) and eye bore size.
- Spring rate should suit the bike and load. A longer shock raises the seat and changes steering.
- Check the mounting rubbers or bushes are included.
- Very cheap shocks often have little damping. Check reviews and the maker's name.

## Fitting notes
- Support the bike so the rear wheel is just off the ground before removing shocks.
- Set both preload adjusters to the same step: [Adjust rear spring preload](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/rear-preload-adjust).
- See [Check the rear shock absorbers](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/rear-shock-check).
