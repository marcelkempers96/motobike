# Sprocket teeth are hooked, sharp or broken

A good sprocket tooth is the same shape on both sides with a rounded top. As the chain wears, the teeth wear into a hook or wave on one side, get thinner and come to a point (sometimes called shark-fin teeth). Worn teeth let the chain ride up and jump, and they wear a new chain quickly. A worn rear sprocket nearly always means the chain and front sprocket are worn too.

## Is it safe to ride?
Ride with care if the teeth are only worn. **Do not ride with broken or missing teeth**: the chain can jump off and lock the wheel.

## Quick checks first
- Look at the rear sprocket teeth from the side, all the way round.
- Look for any broken or chipped teeth.
- Check the chain adjusters: far back means a long, worn chain.

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. Are any teeth broken, chipped or missing?**
- Yes: do not ride. [Replace the chain and sprockets](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-sprocket-replace).
- No: go to step 2.

**2. Are the teeth hooked, wave-shaped, thin or pointed, or worn by about a quarter of a tooth? (K-Light 125 replacement point.)**
- Yes: the rear sprocket is worn. Go to step 3.
- No: go to step 4.

**3. Check the front sprocket and chain ([Inspect sprockets](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/sprocket-inspect), [Check the chain for wear](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-wear-check)). Are they worn too?**
- Almost always yes. Replace the chain and both sprockets together. The K-Light manual warns that a new chain on a worn sprocket, or the other way round, wears out fast. [Replace the chain and sprockets](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-sprocket-replace).

**4. Is the wear only on one side of the teeth (one face polished, the other not)?**
- Yes: the chain runs at an angle. Check the wheel alignment and the sprocket for bending: [Check and adjust chain slack](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-adjust), [Check chain guides, sliders and guards](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-guide-check).
- No: go to step 5.

**5. Does the sprocket wobble when the wheel turns, or move a lot when you rock it against the wheel?**
- Wobble or loose nuts: [Inspect sprockets](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/sprocket-inspect).
- Large rocking movement: worn cush rubbers, see [Clunk or snatch from the rear wheel](/moto-zero/repair/problems/cush-drive-clunk).

**What makes sprockets wear fast:** a dry or dirty chain, a chain set too tight, a misaligned wheel, and fitting a new chain on old sprockets.

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Normal wear with an old chain | Hooked teeth, chain worn | [Replace the chain and sprockets](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-sprocket-replace) |
| Poor chain lubrication | Dry chain, fast wear | [Clean and lubricate the chain](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-clean-lube) |
| Misaligned wheel | One-sided tooth wear | [Check and adjust chain slack](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-adjust) |
| Bent sprocket or loose nuts | Sprocket wobbles | [Inspect sprockets](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/sprocket-inspect) |
| Impact damage | Broken teeth after a stone or chain jump | [Replace the chain and sprockets](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-sprocket-replace) |
