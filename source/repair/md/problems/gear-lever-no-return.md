# Gear lever does not spring back

After each change, the gear lever should return to the middle by itself so it is ready for the next change. If it stays up or down, you have to lift or push it back with your foot, and the next change may be missed. The cause is either outside the engine (lever touching something, a dry pivot, mud, a bent lever) or inside (a broken return spring or bent shift spindle). A simple test tells you which.

## Is it safe to ride?
Ride with care. Gear changes become slow and you may miss a gear when you need it. If the lever will not move at all, see [Stuck in one gear](/moto-zero/repair/problems/stuck-in-gear).

## Quick checks first
- Is the lever bent so it rubs on the engine case, footpeg or chain cover?
- Is mud or a stone packed under the lever?
- On a linkage, does the pedal pivot feel dry or rusty?

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. Is the lever touching anything at the top or bottom of its travel, or is dirt packed around it?**
- Yes: clean it out; straighten or replace a bent lever ([Refit or replace the gear lever](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gear-lever-replace)).
- No: go to step 2.

**2. On a linkage type, is the pedal pivot or a ball joint stiff?**
- Yes: clean and grease it. [Service footpegs and foot pedals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/pedal-peg-service), [Check the gear lever and shift linkage](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearshift-check).
- No, or it is a direct-fit lever: go to step 3.

**3. Mark the lever position, remove it, and turn the bare shift spindle with a spanner (engine off, rear wheel turned by hand). Does the spindle spring back to centre by itself?**
- Yes: the problem is the lever or linkage. Refit it correctly, not touching anything.
- No: the return spring inside the engine is weak or broken, or the spindle is bent. Go to step 4.

**4. Workshop: the return spring and spindle sit behind the clutch-side cover. On the K-Light they can be removed before the crankcase is split.**
- Go to [Assess internal gearbox faults](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearbox-assessment). A broken return spring is a common, cheap part; a bent spindle often follows a fall on the lever.

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Lever touching case, peg or mud | Visible contact or packed dirt | Clean; [Refit or replace the gear lever](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gear-lever-replace) |
| Dry pivot or ball joints | Stiff with lever off the spindle | [Service footpegs and foot pedals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/pedal-peg-service) |
| Bent lever | Lever crooked after a fall | [Refit or replace the gear lever](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gear-lever-replace) |
| Weak or broken return spring | Bare spindle does not return | [Assess internal gearbox faults](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearbox-assessment) |
| Bent shift spindle | Spindle stiff or wobbles | [Assess internal gearbox faults](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearbox-assessment) |
| Broken or weak pawl return spring | The spring pulls the shift pawl and lever back to the middle after each change; if it fails, the lever stays where it was pushed | [Check the gear lever and shift linkage](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearshift-check) |
