# Chain is noisy, clattering or slapping

A healthy chain makes a soft whirr. A dry chain squeaks or grinds, a loose chain slaps against the slider and swingarm on bumps, and a worn chain or sprocket clatters as the rollers ride up the teeth. Noise is the chain's early warning: most causes are cheap to fix if you catch them before the chain and sprockets wear out.

## Is it safe to ride?
Ride with care and check it today. A noisy chain can be close to jumping off or snapping, which can lock the rear wheel.

## Quick checks first
- Engine off, bike on the stand: look at the chain. Dry, orange, or shiny?
- Push the chain up and down at the middle of the lower run. Very loose or very tight?
- Look for marks where the chain hits the swingarm, slider or guard.
- Look at the rear sprocket teeth.
- Check wheel alignment. The front and rear sprockets must be in line, or the chain wears more on one side. Look along the chain to see that it runs straight.

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. Is the chain dry, rusty or dirty?**
- Yes: [Clean and lubricate the chain](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-clean-lube). If the noise goes, that was it.
- No: go to step 2.

**2. Is the slack outside the specified range? (K-Light 125: 20 to 40 mm, bike upright.)**
- Yes: [Check and adjust chain slack](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-adjust).
- No: go to step 3.

**3. Does the slack change a lot as you roll the bike forward (tight in places, loose in others)?**
- Yes: [Chain is tight in some places and slack in others](/moto-zero/repair/problems/chain-tight-spots).
- No: go to step 4.

**4. Are the sprocket teeth hooked, sharp or broken, or can you lift the chain away from the back of the rear sprocket?**
- Yes: [Inspect sprockets](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/sprocket-inspect) and [Check the chain for wear](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-wear-check). Worn parts are replaced as a set.
- No: go to step 5.

**5. Are there rub marks on the slider, swingarm, guard or chain case?**
- Yes: [Chain rubs on the guard, slider or swingarm](/moto-zero/repair/problems/chain-rubbing).
- No: go to step 6.

**6. Is the noise a clunk when you open or close the throttle?**
- Yes: [Clunk or snatch from the rear wheel](/moto-zero/repair/problems/cush-drive-clunk).
- No: go to step 7.

**7. Still noisy with a good chain and sprockets?**
- Spin the rear wheel by hand: a rumble points to the bearings ([Check wheel bearings](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/wheel-bearing-check)). A whine that changes with gear points to the gearbox ([Whining noise in one or more gears](/moto-zero/repair/problems/gearbox-whine)). Check the front sprocket is tight on its shaft.

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dry or dirty chain | Looks dry or rusty, squeaks | [Clean and lubricate the chain](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-clean-lube) |
| Wrong slack | Measured outside range | [Check and adjust chain slack](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-adjust) |
| Worn chain or sprockets | Hooked teeth, chain lifts off sprocket | [Check the chain for wear](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-wear-check) |
| Rubbing on slider or guard | Shiny or cut marks | [Check chain guides, sliders and guards](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-guide-check) |
| Worn cush drive | Clunk on throttle changes | [Check the cush drive rubbers](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/cush-drive-check) |
| Wheel bearing | Rumble when wheel spun | [Check wheel bearings](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/wheel-bearing-check) |
