# Drum brake is weak

A drum brake that needs a lot of force or travel and still slows the bike poorly. In a drum brake, a cam spreads two shoes against the inside of the drum. Weak braking comes from: shoes needing adjustment, worn or glazed shoes, oil or grease on the linings, water in the drum, a stiff cable or cam, or a cam that has turned so far it no longer pushes well.

## Is it safe to ride?
No, until it is adjusted or serviced. A weak rear drum means you are relying on the front alone.

## Quick checks first
- Measure free play at the lever or pedal and compare with your manual.
- Look at the wear indicator pointer on the brake arm with the brake applied: is it past the end of the mark?
- Has the bike just been through deep water or washed? Brake gently a few times to dry it.
- Did you just adjust the chain? Moving the rear wheel changes the brake rod length.
- A twin leading shoe brake has two cams. If they are not adjusted so each shoe touches the drum at the same time, braking is weaker than a single cam brake.
- A cam shaft rusted tight, or lining that has come unglued from the shoe, also gives weak or sticking brakes. Both need the wheel and shoes off.
- After refitting the wheel, spin it, press the brake on hard and hold it, then tighten the axle. This centres the shoes.

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. Is the free play more than your manual allows?**
- Yes: [Adjust a drum brake](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/drum-brake-adjust), then test.
- No: go to step 2.

**2. After adjusting, is the wear pointer past its mark or the adjuster at the end of its thread?**
- Yes: the shoes are worn out. [Service a drum brake](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/drum-brake-service).
- No: go to step 3.

**3. With the brake applied, is the brake arm past a right angle to the rod or cable?**
- Yes: the cam has lost leverage; worn shoes or drum. [Service a drum brake](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/drum-brake-service).
- No: go to step 4.

**4. Is the cable or lever stiff, or slow to return?**
- Yes: much of your effort is lost in friction. [Lubricate control cables](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/cable-lube); replace a frayed cable. See [A control cable is frayed or stiff](/moto-zero/repair/problems/cable-frayed).
- No: go to step 5.

**5. Is there oil or grease near the hub (leaking hub seal, chain lube, over-greased cam)?**
- Yes: oily shoes must be replaced, and the leak fixed. [Service a drum brake](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/drum-brake-service).
- No: go to step 6.

**6. When serviced, are the shoes glazed or the drum glazed, cracked or worn beyond its maximum diameter?**
- Glazed only: clean with fine abrasive paper and refit.
- Worn shoes: replace as a pair.
- Cracked or oversize drum: replace the hub.

**7. Does it get weak only on long hills?**
- Yes: see [Brakes get weaker when hot](/moto-zero/repair/problems/brake-fade).

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Needs adjustment | Too much free play | [Adjust a drum brake](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/drum-brake-adjust) |
| Wet drum | After water; recovers | Dry with gentle brake use |
| Stiff cable or cam | Heavy lever; slow return | [Lubricate control cables](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/cable-lube) |
| Worn shoes | Pointer past mark; adjuster at end | [Service a drum brake](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/drum-brake-service) |
| Oily linings | Oil inside drum | New shoes, fix leak |
| Glazed shoes or drum | Shiny hard surfaces | Clean or replace |
| Worn or cracked drum | Over maximum diameter; cracks | Replace hub |
