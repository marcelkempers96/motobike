# Handlebar is not straight when the wheel is straight

When you ride in a straight line, the handlebar is turned a little to one side, or when you stand in front the bar is not square with the front wheel. This usually happens after a small fall, a knock against a wall, or hitting a pothole: the fork legs twist in their clamps, or the bar turns in its mounts. It can also mean a bent bar, bent forks, a bent yoke (the clamps that hold the forks), or a rear wheel out of line that makes you steer to compensate.

## Is it safe to ride?
No, until you have checked for bent parts. If only the forks have twisted in the clamps and nothing is bent, it can be aligned at home.

## Quick checks first
- Was the bike dropped or hit? When?
- Look along each fork tube: straight?
- Are both fork legs at the same height above the top clamp?
- Is the handlebar itself bent (compare left and right grip heights and angles)?
- Look at the chain adjuster marks on both sides.
- To realign a twisted fork, loosen the pinch bolts only a little, so the legs can twist in the clamps but not slide, and turn the bars gently into line. Retighten to the value in your bike's manual.
- If the bars cannot be lined up this way, the frame, forks, axle or yokes may be bent. See [Align the front wheel, forks and handlebar](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/front-end-align).

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. Has the bike been crashed, or is any part visibly bent?**
- Yes: **do not ride.** Go to [The bike has been dropped or crashed](/moto-zero/repair/problems/crash-check) and [Inspect the frame after a crash](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/frame-crash-inspect). Bent forks, yokes and frames are not straightened at home.
- No: go to step 2.

**2. Is the handlebar itself bent?**
- Yes: replace the handlebar. Bending it back weakens the tube.
- No: go to step 3.

**3. Has the bar turned in its clamps or rubber mounts?**
- Yes: square it and tighten to the model torque. Rubber-mounted bars (as on the K-Light 125) can twist if a mount rubber is torn. See [Align the front wheel, forks and handlebar](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/front-end-align).
- No: go to step 4.

**4. Hold the front wheel between your knees and push on the bar. Are the forks twisted in the clamps?**
- Yes: [Align the front wheel, forks and handlebar](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/front-end-align): loosen the clamps and axle, straighten, retighten in order.
- No: go to step 5.

**5. Are the chain adjuster marks different on each side, so the rear wheel points off line?**
- Yes: realign with [Check and adjust chain slack](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-adjust).
- No: go to step 6.

**6. After alignment, do the bars still sit crooked, or do the forks feel stiff?**
- Yes: a fork tube or yoke is bent. Workshop. See [Fork legs are twisted or uneven](/moto-zero/repair/problems/fork-twisted).

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Forks twisted in clamps | Wheel turns against bar with force | [Align the front wheel, forks and handlebar](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/front-end-align) |
| Bar turned in clamps or mounts | Bar not square to top clamp | Square and tighten |
| Bent handlebar | Grips at different angles | Replace bar |
| Rear wheel out of line | Adjuster marks differ | [Check and adjust chain slack](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-adjust) |
| Bent fork, yoke or frame | After crash; will not align | [Inspect the frame after a crash](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/frame-crash-inspect) |
