# Chain is tight in some places and slack in others

When you roll the bike and check the slack at different points, it changes: tight at one spot, loose a little further on. This happens when the chain has worn unevenly, when some links have gone stiff, or when a sprocket is bent or sits off-centre. The danger is setting the slack at a loose spot: at the tight spot the chain is then far too tight, which strains the gearbox output bearing and the wheel bearing and can make the wheel drag.

## Is it safe to ride?
Ride with care, after you have set the slack at the tightest point. Do not ride with the chain tight at any point.

## Quick checks first
- Bike upright, in neutral: measure slack, roll forward a little, measure again, all the way round the chain.
- Watch the links go round the rear sprocket: do any stay bent?
- Watch the rear sprocket edge as the wheel turns: does it wobble?

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. Does the slack vary as you roll the chain round?**
- Yes: mark the tightest point with chalk. Go to step 2.
- No: the chain is even; if it is noisy, see [Chain is noisy, clattering or slapping](/moto-zero/repair/problems/chain-noise).

**2. Do one or more links stay bent (kinked) as they leave the rear sprocket?**
- Yes: [Clean and lubricate the chain](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-clean-lube), working oil into the stiff links. If they stay stiff, the K-Light manual says replace the chain. See [Chain is dry, rusty or has stiff links](/moto-zero/repair/problems/chain-dry-rusty).
- No: go to step 3.

**3. Does the rear sprocket wobble, or are its nuts loose?**
- Yes: [Inspect sprockets](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/sprocket-inspect). Loose nuts are tightened in a criss-cross pattern (K-Light 125: 45 to 50 N·m, in two or three passes). A bent sprocket is replaced with the chain.
- No: go to step 4.

**4. Is the chain old, with the adjusters near the end of their range?**
- Yes: it has worn unevenly. [Check the chain for wear](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-wear-check), then [Replace the chain and sprockets](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-sprocket-replace).
- No: go to step 5.

**5. Set the slack at the tightest point. Is it in range there? (K-Light 125: 20 to 40 mm.)**
- [Check and adjust chain slack](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-adjust). Then roll the chain round again: no point should be tighter than the lower limit.

**What happens if you ignore it:** the tight spot pulls hard on the gearbox output shaft and wheel bearings every turn, makes the bike feel held back ([Bike feels held back](/moto-zero/repair/problems/bike-feels-held-back)), and the loose spot can let the chain jump.

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Stiff links | Links stay bent off the sprocket | [Clean and lubricate the chain](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-clean-lube) or replace |
| Uneven chain wear | Old chain, adjusters far back | [Replace the chain and sprockets](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-sprocket-replace) |
| Bent sprocket or loose nuts | Sprocket wobbles | [Inspect sprockets](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/sprocket-inspect) |
| Slack set at a loose point | Tight spot below range | [Check and adjust chain slack](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/chain-adjust) |
