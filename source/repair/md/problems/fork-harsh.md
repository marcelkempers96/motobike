# Front forks feel harsh over bumps

Every small bump is felt sharply through the handlebar, as if the forks do not move enough. Possible causes: front tyre pressure too high, forks binding because they are twisted in the clamps or a tube is bent, too much oil or oil that is too thick, dirty or dry seals and bushes, or the fork is actually bottoming on a bigger bump (which feels like a hard knock).

## Is it safe to ride?
Ride with care. A fork that binds can lose grip over bumps in corners.

## Quick checks first
- Check front tyre pressure (K-Light 125: 190 ± 10 kPa cold).
- Hold the front brake and push the bars down: smooth, or does it stick?
- Was the fork serviced recently? Too much oil or the wrong grade is a common cause.
- Was the bike dropped recently?
- Thicker oil makes the fork action stiffer. Check that the oil grade and level match the manual.

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. Is the front tyre pressure too high?**
- Yes: set it with [Check tyre pressure](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/tyre-pressure-check).
- No: go to step 2.

**2. When you push the forks down with the brake on, do they stick, then jump?**
- Yes: they are binding. Go to [Front forks feel sticky or stiff](/moto-zero/repair/problems/fork-sticky).
- No: go to step 3.

**3. Is the harshness a hard knock on bigger bumps only?**
- Yes: the fork is bottoming. Go to [Front forks bottom out](/moto-zero/repair/problems/fork-bottoming).
- No: go to step 4.

**4. Were the forks recently serviced or the oil changed?**
- Yes: too much oil, or oil thicker than specified, makes the fork harsh. Ask the workshop to check the amount and grade against the manual (K-Light 125: 285 ± 2 ml per leg, 46#). See [Replace fork oil and seals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fork-oil-seals).
- No: go to step 5.

**5. Are the dust seals dry and dirty, or the tubes rusty?**
- Yes: clean the tubes and seals with [Clean fork seals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fork-seal-clean). Rusty tubes need a workshop.
- No: go to step 6.

**6. Does the bike sit high at the front, with very little sag?**
- Check with [Measure suspension sag](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/sag-measure). Very little sag with a normal rider can mean wrong springs or spacers were fitted. Workshop.

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Tyre pressure too high | Gauge reading | [Check tyre pressure](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/tyre-pressure-check) |
| Forks binding (twisted, bent) | Sticky movement | [Align the front wheel, forks and handlebar](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/front-end-align) |
| Too much or too thick oil | After service | [Replace fork oil and seals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fork-oil-seals) |
| Dry, dirty seals | Dirt at seals | [Clean fork seals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fork-seal-clean) |
| Bottoming on big bumps | Hard knock | See [Front forks bottom out](/moto-zero/repair/problems/fork-bottoming) |
| Wrong springs or spacers | Very little sag | Workshop |
