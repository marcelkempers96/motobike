# Popping or banging from the exhaust when slowing down

When you close the throttle, the engine gets very little fuel. If extra air gets into the exhaust (through a leaking joint or a loose silencer), or if the mixture is too lean, unburned fuel can light in the hot pipe and pop or bang. A few soft pops on a bike with a free-flowing aftermarket exhaust are common. Loud bangs, or new popping after work on the exhaust or carburettor, point to a leak or a mixture fault. Some bikes have an air injection system that adds air to the exhaust for emissions; a fault in it can also cause popping.

## Is it safe to ride?
Yes with care. Get it checked; a lean engine runs hot and loud bangs can damage the silencer.

## Quick checks first
- Is there a ticking or puffing at the exhaust joint near the head?
- Has the exhaust or silencer been changed, or the silencer baffle removed?
- Has the air filter, carburettor or intake rubber been worked on?
- Fuel-injected bike: is the warning light on?

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. With the engine cold, look and listen at the exhaust joints. Any soot streaks, ticking or a loose silencer clamp?**
- Yes: fix it with [Find and fix an exhaust leak](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/exhaust-leak-check). This is the most common cause.
- No: go to step 2.

**2. Has the exhaust been replaced with an open or louder one?**
- Yes: some popping is normal with less restriction. A fuel-injected bike may need its fuel map matched by the dealer. On a carburettor bike the mixture may need checking. Go to step 4.
- No: go to step 3.

**3. Is there an intake air leak: cracked intake rubber, loose clamp, split vacuum hose? Check with [Find an intake air leak](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/intake-leak-test).**
- Yes: fix it. An air leak makes the mixture lean.
- No: go to step 4.

**4. Carburettor bike: is the idle mixture or idle speed set wrongly? Check with [Adjust carburettor idle speed and mixture](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/carb-idle-adjust).**
- Fuel-injected: read codes with [Read engine fault codes (fuel injection)](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/efi-code-read). Go to step 5.

**5. Four-stroke: is the valve clearance correct? Too little clearance can hold the exhaust valve slightly open. Check with [Check and adjust valve clearance](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/valve-clearance).**
- Out of range: set it.
- Correct: go to step 6.

**6. Does it also pop when starting or under load?**
- Yes: ignition timing or a loose pick-up may be involved. The K-Light manual links backfire to a loose coil or reluctor ring. Ask for [Check the ignition timing](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/ignition-timing-check). See [Pops or bangs when trying to start](/moto-zero/repair/problems/backfire-on-start).
- No: see [Engine runs hot and lean, pops and pinks](/moto-zero/repair/problems/runs-lean).

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Exhaust leak at head or silencer joint | Soot, ticking | [Find and fix an exhaust leak](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/exhaust-leak-check) |
| Intake air leak | Cracked rubber, hissing | [Find an intake air leak](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/intake-leak-test) |
| Lean idle mixture | Pops on closing throttle, hanging revs | [Adjust carburettor idle speed and mixture](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/carb-idle-adjust) |
| Aftermarket open exhaust | Recent change | [Read engine fault codes (fuel injection)](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/efi-code-read) |
| Tight valve clearance | Feeler gauge | [Check and adjust valve clearance](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/valve-clearance) |
| Ignition fault | Pops on start too | [Check the ignition timing](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/ignition-timing-check) |
