# Rumbling or growling from inside the engine

A rough rumble or growl that rises and falls with engine speed often comes from a worn crankshaft main bearing, a gearbox bearing or the clutch. Unlike a knock, it is a continuous rough sound. Main bearings wear from dirty or low oil, water in the oil, or age. Sometimes the sound is outside the engine: a wheel bearing, the chain or a dry sprocket. Finding where it comes from is the key.

## Is it safe to ride?
Do not ride until checked. A failing main bearing can let the crankshaft move and destroy the engine, and a seizing bearing can lock the rear wheel.

## Quick checks first
- Oil level and colour. Milky or very dirty oil wears bearings.
- Does the noise change when you pull the clutch lever in, at idle in neutral?
- Does it happen with the engine running and the bike still, or only when moving?
- Is the chain dry or the rear wheel noisy when spun by hand?
- A bearing that binds, then seems to free itself when the shaft is turned back and forth, has not recovered. Do not start the engine.

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. Bike on the centre stand, engine off. Spin the rear wheel by hand (neutral). Is there a rumble or roughness?**
- Yes: the noise is in the wheel or drive, not the engine. Go to [Rumbling or grinding from a wheel](/moto-zero/repair/problems/wheel-bearing-noise) and check the chain with [Chain is noisy, clattering or slapping](/moto-zero/repair/problems/chain-noise).
- No: go to step 2.

**2. Engine running in neutral, bike still. Is the rumble there?**
- No, only when riding: likely wheel, chain, gearbox under load or cush drive. See [Whining noise in one or more gears](/moto-zero/repair/problems/gearbox-whine).
- Yes: go to step 3.

**3. Pull the clutch lever in and out at idle. Does the noise change clearly?**
- Yes: it is likely in the clutch or primary drive. Go to [Rattle or whir from the clutch that changes when the lever is pulled](/moto-zero/repair/problems/clutch-noise).
- No: go to step 4.

**4. Listen with a long screwdriver on the left cover (generator), the right cover (clutch), and the crankcase below the cylinder. Is it loudest on the crankcase near the crankshaft ends?**
- Yes: main bearing wear is likely. Go to step 5.
- Loudest on the left cover with a scraping sound: the generator rotor may be touching the stator; workshop.

**5. Is the oil low, milky or full of metal? Check with [Check the engine oil level](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/oil-level-check) and [Inspect drained oil for clues](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/oil-inspect).**
- Yes: bearing damage is likely. Go to [Assess bottom end and internal engine damage](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/engine-internal-assessment).
- No: still ask the workshop to check. Main bearings on many small engines come with the crankshaft assembly.

**6. Workshop check.** The workshop splits the crankcase and checks main bearings, crankshaft runout and gearbox bearings. The K-Light manual replaces the crank assembly if the main bearings are noisy or loose.

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Wheel bearing or chain (not engine) | Noise when spinning the wheel | [Check wheel bearings](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/wheel-bearing-check) |
| Clutch or primary drive | Changes with lever | [Inspect and replace clutch plates and springs](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/clutch-plate-service) |
| Worn crankshaft main bearing | Loudest at crankcase, oil dirty | [Assess bottom end and internal engine damage](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/engine-internal-assessment) |
| Gearbox bearing | Only in gear under load | [Assess internal gearbox faults](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearbox-assessment) |
| Generator rotor rubbing | Scrape at left cover | [Assess bottom end and internal engine damage](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/engine-internal-assessment) |
