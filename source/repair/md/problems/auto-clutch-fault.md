# Semi-automatic clutch (step-through) slips or engages roughly

Step-through commuters without a clutch lever use an automatic clutch: a centrifugal part that grips as revs rise, plus a clutch that the gear pedal releases while you press it. Faults show as slipping (revs rise but the bike is slow in every gear), a lurch or bang when you change gear, creeping at idle, or a jerky take-off. Start with oil, idle speed and how the pedal is used. Internal clutch work is a workshop job.

## Is it safe to ride?
Ride with care. A lurch when selecting a gear can push you into a junction, and slipping leaves you short of power when pulling out. Hold the brakes when changing gear at a standstill.

## Quick checks first
- Check the engine oil level; on most step-throughs the clutch runs in it.
- Listen to the idle: is it faster than usual?
- Close the throttle fully before each gear change and press the pedal through its full travel.
- Check the gear pedal is not bent and does not touch the footpeg.
- Many step-through bikes have a small lever on the gear shaft that fully frees the clutch while the gear is changed. Check that the lever and its linkage are not bent or loose.
- Automatic clutches use weights that press plates together (plate type), or spring-held shoes that swing out against a drum (shoe type). In both, worn plates, shoes or springs are replaced, not adjusted.

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. Is the oil level correct, and is the oil fresh and of the grade in your manual?**
- No: [Check the engine oil level](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/oil-level-check); if old or of unknown type, [Change the engine oil](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/engine-oil-service).
- Yes: go to step 2.

**2. Is the idle speed too high, or does the bike creep and lurch only when a gear is selected at idle?**
- Yes: a high idle makes the centrifugal clutch grip at idle. See [Idle speed is too high](/moto-zero/repair/problems/high-idle); on carburettor bikes [Adjust carburettor idle speed and mixture](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/carb-idle-adjust).
- No: go to step 3.

**3. Does the jerk happen when you change gear with the throttle still open, or with a quick tap on the pedal?**
- Yes: close the throttle fully, press the pedal firmly through its whole travel, hold briefly, then release smoothly and open the throttle.
- No: go to step 4.

**4. Does the gear pedal move its full stroke freely and spring back, without slack on its shaft?**
- No: [Check the gear lever and shift linkage](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearshift-check).
- Yes: go to step 5.

**5. Does it still slip in every gear, or engage roughly, after steps 1 to 4?**
- Yes: the external clutch adjuster may be out of setting, or the shoes, plates or springs are worn. Go to [Check a semi-automatic or centrifugal clutch](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/auto-clutch-check). Only change the adjuster with your manual's exact steps.

**What happens if you ignore it:** a slipping automatic clutch overheats the oil and wears faster, and rough engagement hammers the gearbox dogs.

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Low, old or wrong oil | Level low, dark, unknown grade | [Change the engine oil](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/engine-oil-service) |
| Idle too high | Creeps or lurches at idle in gear | [Idle speed is too high](/moto-zero/repair/problems/high-idle) |
| Riding technique | Jerk only with throttle open or quick pedal taps | Close throttle, full pedal stroke |
| Pedal or linkage play | Pedal loose, bent or short of travel | [Check the gear lever and shift linkage](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/gearshift-check) |
| Adjuster out of setting | Changes after adjuster set per manual | [Check a semi-automatic or centrifugal clutch](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/auto-clutch-check) |
| Worn shoes, plates or springs | Persists after all the above | [Check a semi-automatic or centrifugal clutch](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/auto-clutch-check) |
