# Knocking or squeaking from the front forks

A knock, clunk, squeak or creak from the front when riding over bumps or pushing the forks down. The K-Light 125 manual lists two causes first: a cover rubbing and loose bolts. Other causes are steering head bearing play, worn fork bushes, low oil (the fork reaches its end stop), topping out (the fork extends fully with a clack, from low oil or a broken rebound spring), and dry dust seals that squeak.

## Is it safe to ride?
Ride with care while you find it. If the noise is a clunk when braking, or anything is loose, **do not ride**: see [Clunk from the front when braking](/moto-zero/repair/problems/front-clunk-braking).

## Quick checks first
- Push the forks down with the brake on and listen: does the noise come from the legs, the head tube, the mudguard or the headlight?
- Check mudguard, headlight and cover bolts.
- Look for oil on the tubes.
- Is it a squeak (seal or dry part) or a knock (loose part)?

## Diagnose it step by step

**1. Is a mudguard, headlight, cover or cable rubbing or loose?**
- Yes: tighten or fix it. See [Check chassis fasteners](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fastener-check) and [Rattling from panels, mudguards or the seat](/moto-zero/repair/problems/bodywork-rattle).
- No: go to step 2.

**2. Are the axle, pinch bolts, caliper bolts and fork clamp bolts tight?**
- No: tighten to model torques. [Check chassis fasteners](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fastener-check).
- Yes: go to step 3.

**3. With the brake held and the bike rocked, is there a clunk at the head tube?**
- Yes: [Check the steering head bearings](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/steering-bearing-check).
- No: go to step 4.

**4. Is it a squeak when the forks move, with dry or dirty dust seals?**
- Yes: [Clean fork seals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fork-seal-clean).
- No: go to step 5.

**5. Is it a knock at full compression (big bumps) or a clack at full extension (lifting the front or after a dip)?**
- Full compression: low oil or tired springs. See [Front forks bottom out](/moto-zero/repair/problems/fork-bottoming).
- Full extension: low oil or a broken rebound spring. Workshop: [Replace fork oil and seals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fork-oil-seals).
- Neither: go to step 6.

**6. Lift the front wheel. Can you move a fork slider forward and back on its tube?**
- Yes: worn fork bushes. [Replace fork oil and seals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fork-oil-seals), with new bushes.
- No: have a workshop check inside the forks.

## Causes, most likely first

| Cause | How to confirm | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Cover, mudguard or headlight rubbing | Noise moves when you hold the part | Tighten or pad the part |
| Loose bolts | Fastener moves | [Check chassis fasteners](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fastener-check) |
| Dry dust seals | Squeak as forks move | [Clean fork seals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fork-seal-clean) |
| Steering head play | Clunk at head tube | [Check the steering head bearings](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/steering-bearing-check) |
| Low oil, topping or bottoming out | Knock at end of travel | [Replace fork oil and seals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fork-oil-seals) |
| Worn fork bushes | Slider moves on tube | [Replace fork oil and seals](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/fork-oil-seals) |
