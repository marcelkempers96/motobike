# Adjust carburettor idle speed and mixture

**Home mechanic (basic tools)** · Time: 20 to 40 min (estimate) (estimate)


A carburettor has two idle screws. The **throttle stop screw** (idle speed screw) holds the slide open a little to set how fast the engine idles. The **pilot screw** sets the idle mixture: on some carburettors it adjusts air, on others fuel. Adjustment is seldom needed. Do it only after the basics are right, because a dirty air filter, a worn plug, wrong valve clearance or an air leak all upset idle, and turning screws will not cure them.

**Fuel-injected bikes:** do not turn the idle screw on the throttle body. The ECU (engine control unit) controls idle by itself (K-Light 125 manual, PDF page 258). Go to [Reset the idle after throttle body work](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/idle-relearn) instead.

## Safety

- Run the engine outdoors only. Exhaust gas contains carbon monoxide, which kills in closed rooms.
- The exhaust and engine get very hot; keep hands and cables clear.
- Do not adjust while riding. Many countries limit idle mixture changes for emissions; some pilot screws carry an anti-tamper cap.

## You need

- Your manual's idle speed and pilot screw starting setting. These are model-specific; this page gives no universal number.
- A tachometer (rev counter) if the bike has none. Idle by ear is not accurate enough.

## Before you start

- Clean or replace the [air filter](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/air-filter-service).
- Check the [spark plug](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/spark-plug-service) and [valve clearance](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/valve-clearance) are correct.
- Set [throttle free play](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/throttle-cable-adjust) so the slide closes fully.
- Check for [intake air leaks](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/intake-leak-test).

## Steps

1. Ride or run the engine until it is fully warm (normal temperature, choke fully off).
2. Put the bike on its centre stand in neutral, outdoors. Air-cooled engines: point a fan at the cylinder if you will idle for long.
3. Turn the throttle stop screw until the engine idles at the speed in your manual.
4. Turn the pilot screw slowly **in** until the idle speed starts to drop or becomes rough. Note the position.
5. Turn it slowly **out** until the idle again starts to drop or becomes rough. Note this position.
6. Set the pilot screw halfway between the two points. This is usually the fastest, smoothest idle. Make changes of a quarter turn or less, and wait a few seconds after each.
7. Reset idle speed with the throttle stop screw.
8. Blip the throttle two or three times. The engine should return to the same idle within a second or two.

## More tips
- An air screw controls how much air mixes with the idle fuel. A fuel screw meters the fuel itself. They turn in opposite senses, so check which type you have first.
- If the idle is high, then drops and dies, check for an air leak at the manifold. See [Test for an intake air leak](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/intake-leak-test).
- Tight valve clearances also upset the idle.
- A cap or plug may cover the screw. It may be restricted to remove it where you live.
- For a different idle jet size, see [Change the idle jet and read the plug](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/idle-jet-change).

## Check your work

- Steady idle at the manual speed, warm, with no hunting.
- Turn the handlebars fully left and right: idle must not change. If it rises, the throttle cable is too tight or badly routed.
- Clean, quick throttle response from idle.

## If it did not fix the problem

- The pilot screw has no effect: the pilot jet is blocked. See [Clean the carburettor](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/carburettor-clean).
- Idle hunts or stays high: see [Idle speed rises and falls on its own](/moto-zero/repair/problems/hunting-idle) and [Idle speed is too high](/moto-zero/repair/problems/high-idle).
- You must turn the pilot screw far out to get a smooth idle: suspect an air leak or a lean pilot circuit. See [Engine runs hot and lean](/moto-zero/repair/problems/runs-lean).
