# Bleed a hydraulic clutch

**Home mechanic (basic tools)** · Time: 30 to 45 min (estimate)


Some bikes use fluid, not a cable, to work the clutch. The lever pushes a small master cylinder; fluid moves to a slave cylinder at the engine, which releases the clutch. Air in the system squashes instead of pushing, so the clutch does not release fully: the bike creeps in gear and neutral is hard to find. Bleeding pushes the air out through a small valve (the bleed nipple) on the slave cylinder. Most small commuter bikes, including the K-Light 125, use a cable; this page is for bikes with a reservoir on the clutch lever.

## Safety
- Check the reservoir cap. It tells you the fluid: brake fluid (often DOT 4) or mineral oil. **Never mix them.** The wrong fluid destroys the seals.
- Brake fluid hurts eyes and skin and strips paint almost at once. Wear glasses and gloves, cover paint with plastic sheet, and wash spills off with water.
- Never reuse old fluid. Use a sealed, fresh bottle.

## You need
- Fresh fluid of the type on the cap.
- A ring spanner that fits the bleed nipple (an open spanner rounds it).
- About 60 cm of clear tube that fits tightly on the nipple, and a bottle to catch fluid.

## Steps
1. Turn the handlebar so the clutch reservoir is level. Cover the tank and paint.
2. Clean the cap, remove it and the rubber diaphragm. Wipe the diaphragm clean; do not wash it in solvent.
3. Top up the reservoir with fresh fluid. Do not fill above the upper mark.
4. Fit the ring spanner on the bleed nipple at the slave cylinder, then push the clear tube onto the nipple and put the other end in the bottle with a little fluid in it.
5. Squeeze the clutch lever and hold it in.
6. Open the bleed nipple a little (about half a turn). Fluid and air come out and the lever goes towards the bar.
7. Close the nipple while still holding the lever.
8. Release the lever slowly.
9. Repeat steps 5 to 8 until no bubbles come down the tube. Watch the reservoir every two or three strokes. If it runs low, air goes back in and you must start again.
10. Close the nipple firmly but do not overtighten; use the torque in your manual. Remove the tube.
11. Top up the reservoir to the level mark, refit the diaphragm and cap.

## Check your work
- The lever feels firm, with a clear point where the clutch starts to release.
- Engine running, lever pulled: first gear goes in quietly and the bike does not creep.
- No fluid at the nipple, the hose joints or the slave cylinder after a few lever pulls.

## If it did not fix the problem
- Air keeps returning or the fluid level drops: there is a leak, often at the slave cylinder seal or master cylinder. This needs a workshop. Do not ride with a clutch that will not release.
- Lever firm but the clutch still drags: [Clutch drags](/moto-zero/repair/problems/clutch-drag).
- For more on handling fluid and bleeding, see [Replace brake fluid and bleed the brakes](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/brake-bleed); the method is the same.
