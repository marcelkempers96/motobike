# Road test to diagnose a fault

**Rider job (no special tools)** · Time: 15 to 30 min (estimate) (estimate)


Many running faults only show up on the road: under load, at a certain speed, when hot, or over bumps. A planned road test lets you see exactly **when** the fault happens. That information narrows the diagnosis more than any guess. Do a short test before a repair (to confirm the fault) and after it (to prove the fix).

## Safety

- Most serious workshop injuries happen on test rides. Wear a helmet and protective clothing every time, even for a short test.
- Do the [Pre-ride check](/moto-zero/repair/repairs/pre-ride-check) first: brakes, tyres, lights, throttle snap-back, chain. Never road test a bike with weak brakes, a fuel leak or a throttle that sticks.
- Choose a quiet, legal route. Watch the traffic, not the bike. A road test is not a speed test.
- If the engine cuts out, signal, move to the side and stop safely before you look at anything.
- Do not test a customer's bike without permission.

## Steps

**Plan the test**

1. Ask the rider or decide yourself: what exactly happens, and when? Cold or hot? At idle, in the middle, or at full throttle? In which gear? Uphill, with a passenger, in rain, over bumps?
2. Check tyre pressure and that the wheels spin freely on the stand (a dragging brake feels like a weak engine; the K-Light 125 power fault chart starts here).
3. Note the fuel level, the warning lights at key-on, and the odometer.

**During the test**

4. Start cold if the fault is a cold one. Note how it starts and idles.
5. Pull away gently in first gear. Note any hesitation or judder.
6. Accelerate through the gears at part throttle, then at full throttle in a middle gear. Listen for misfire, pinking (a light metallic rattle) or bogging.
7. Hold a steady speed for a minute in top gear. Feel for surging or jerking.
8. Roll the throttle off. Note popping from the exhaust and whether the revs fall back cleanly.
9. Climb a hill if the fault is power-related. Watch whether engine revs rise without the bike speeding up (clutch or CVT slip).
10. Ride long enough to reach full temperature, then repeat the step where the fault appeared. Note if it changes when hot.
11. Ride over a rough patch at low speed if the fault is a cut-out.
12. Stop and idle for a minute when hot. Note any stalling or rising idle.

**After the test**

13. Write down what happened at each step while you remember it.
14. Check for new leaks, smells and warning lights. Feel nothing hot with bare hands.
15. Use your notes to choose the problem page: for example [Misfires when accelerating hard](/moto-zero/repair/problems/misfire-under-load), [Power fades as the engine gets hot](/moto-zero/repair/problems/power-fades-hot) or [Engine revs rise but the bike does not speed up](/moto-zero/repair/problems/revs-without-speed).

## Check your work

- After a repair, the same route and conditions no longer bring back the fault.
- No new leaks, noises or warning lights after the ride.
