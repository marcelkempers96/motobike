# Moto Zero

**An open technical course on how motorcycles work**, from the first combustion stroke to the battery management system of an electric motorcycle. The course follows the motorcycle from its anatomy and physics through the four-stroke and two-stroke engine, fuel, ignition and electrics, and drivetrain and chassis, to electric drive. Every chapter closes with review questions and worked answers, and every module contains workshop job sheets with tools, reference values and pass criteria.

The course is written for **technician trainees, engineering and transitions students, researchers, fleet operators and riders** who want to understand the machine well enough to explain it, specify it and repair it. Reference values come from a current fuel-injected 125 cc single (the K-Light 125) so that every calculation and service limit maps onto a real machine.

## Explore the machine

**Interactive controls.** A rider's-eye cockpit of a 125 cc road motorcycle in which every control works: turn the key, set the stop switch to RUN, find neutral, press the starter, then pull the clutch, select first and open the throttle while the tachometer, speedometer and warning lamps respond. [Open the controls simulator](/moto-zero/controls)

**3D models.** Three complete motorcycles (a classic air-cooled four-stroke, a two-stroke motocross bike and an electric scrambler) and 53 individual components, from the crankshaft, valves and carburettor to the PMSM motor, inverter, wheels, forks and wiring harness. Every model can be rotated, and on the full bikes each system can be highlighted: powertrain, transmission, chassis, frame and body, wheels, electrical and wiring. [Open the 3D viewer](/moto-zero/viewer)

**Illustrations by system.** Blueprint plates for every category, from the four-stroke cycle and the wet clutch to wheel types, frame types, the wiring harness and the electric drive, each with labelled, hoverable parts.

| System | What you can study |
|---|---|
| **Powertrain** | Engine sections, four-stroke and two-stroke cycles, valvetrain, lubrication, fuel injection, carburettor, exhausts, two-stroke ports, electric motor |
| **Transmission** | Wet multiplate clutch, gearbox and shift mechanism, chain, belt and shaft final drives, scooter CVT |
| **Chassis** | Steering geometry, forks and shocks, brakes, swingarm, handlebar and controls |

## How the course is built

**Every chapter** opens with learning objectives and closes with eight to twelve review questions that mix recall, calculation and fault diagnosis, followed by worked answers.

**Job sheets** give step-by-step workshop procedures with the tools, reference values and pass criteria, from reading a micrometer to bleeding a hydraulic brake and isolating a high-voltage battery.

**Explorer exercises** link the text to the interactive [Motorcycle Anatomy explorer](https://www.transitionslab.org/motorcycle-anatomy): set bore, stroke, compression ratio and engine speed and check the numbers against the worked examples, step through the four-stroke and two-stroke cycles, and find each of the 30 numbered assemblies in the exploded diagram.

**Illustrations** are original technical plates drawn for the course in a single blueprint style, complemented by photorealistic 3D renders of key parts (crank assembly, valve set, spark plug, chain and sprocket, disc brake, battery module) modelled for the course. Both can be reused in teaching material under the same licence.

## Suggested learning path

1. **Module 01** for everyone, then the explorer's exploded diagram.
2. **Modules 02 and 03** for combustion machines, in that order.
3. **Module 04** for every machine, combustion or electric.
4. **Module 05** after Module 03 chapter 3.4 (battery, charging and starting) and chapter 3.6 (electrical diagnosis).
